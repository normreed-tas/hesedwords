Idle

ἀργός

argos · Greek adjective

When Jesus warns that we will give account for every idle word (Matthew 12:36), the word beneath the English is argos — and it says more than “careless.”

The Shape of the Word

Argos is a compound: the negating a- joined to ergon, “work.” Literally, without work — not working, unemployed, standing by. From there its meanings fan out: idle, inactive, unproductive, barren, ineffective. It is the word for a field left unsown, a labourer not yet hired, a tool that does nothing. An idle word, then, is not merely a loose one. It is a workless one — a word that carries nothing, does nothing, employs nothing.

Where Scripture Uses It

Matthew 12:36 Every idle word will be brought to account. The word is weighed not by its loudness but by its emptiness — whether it did any work at all.
Matthew 20:3, 6 The labourers stand idle in the marketplace — not lazy, but unhired, unemployed, waiting to be put to work. Here argos is pure potential, unspent.
1 Timothy 5:13 Younger widows are warned against learning to be idle, drifting house to house. Idleness here breeds the very thing our study circles — words that should not have been spoken.
Titus 1:12 The Cretans, in the prophet’s own barb, are “lazy gluttons” — argoi, bellies that consume and produce nothing.
2 Peter 1:8 If faith, virtue, knowledge and the rest increase, they keep us from being idle (argos) and unfruitful. Here the word stands beside “unfruitful” as its twin — the barren field again.
The thread that runs through every use is not chatter but barrenness. To be argos is to take up room and yield nothing — a field, a labourer, a belly, a word.

Why It Sits Well

Set against Ezekiel, the word turns luminous. His season of silence was the very opposite of argos. He said little, yet nothing about him was workless — his stillness laboured, his life bore the message his lips were not free to speak. So the call to keep our words few is not a call to say less for its own sake. It is a call to spend no idle word: to let the few we speak actually work.
