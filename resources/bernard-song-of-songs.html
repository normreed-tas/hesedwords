Bernard of Clairvaux on the Song of Songs

Bernard preached these eighty-six sermons to the monks of Clairvaux across more than two decades, returning again and again to a single book as though it could never be exhausted. He read the Song neither as history nor as allegory in the loose sense, but as the record of an experience available, in some measure, to anyone who has tasted what he calls the kiss of Christ. He never reached beyond the third chapter; he died before he could finish. What survives is the longest sustained meditation on divine intimacy in Western monastic literature.

Let Him kiss me with the kisses of His mouth. Bernard hears this as the patriarchs’ own impatience — weary of a God who comes only through stammering prophets, and asking instead for the thing itself, unmediated.
