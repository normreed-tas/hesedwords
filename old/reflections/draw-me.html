Draw Me

A reflection on Song of Songs 1:4

Norm Reed · July 2026

The Fire That Would Not Draw

Draw me after you; let us run.

Song of Songs 1:4 · ESV

In my home at Findon we had an old brick fireplace, and later a cast iron box fire that replaced it. I would wake early and find the coals still faintly alive from the night before — not out, but not burning either. Just holding on.

You cannot argue a fire back to life. I learned that at that hearth. I would blow on the coals, feed in kindling, and still nothing would properly catch until the fire began to draw — until air was pulled in at the bottom and pulled up through the chimney, faster and faster, the whole thing feeding on its own hunger for more air. In the old brick fireplace I would hold a sheet of paper across the opening, just a small gap left at the bottom, and the air would rush in through that gap and the fire would catch it and roar. That roar was the sound of a fire that had started drawing.

I did not create that draw. I could only make the conditions for it and wait for it to begin.

Not My Work

Jesus said it plainly: no one can come to me unless the Father draws him. I have read that verse for years the way you read a doctrine — true, settled, filed away. Reading “draw me” in the Song, this time, it stopped being doctrine and became recognition.

She cannot work this up. She cannot assemble the ingredients of nearness to Him by trying harder. There is no kindling she can gather that will make the fire draw on its own. All she can do — all any of us can do — is ask for the breath that starts the pull. Draw me.

✦   ✦   ✦

Selah

I am enjoying this journey through the Song of Songs. Leaning in, trying to grasp the heart of the Shulammite. I had already found my longing for the kiss expressing the desire of my heart. But this morning, reading the words “draw me,” I noticed something had changed.

She is no longer talking about what she wants. She is summing up, in two simple words, the request to make.

They are words to sit with. Words to reflect on. But more than that, they are words to voice. In prayer, to the Father — please draw me. Deeper. More fully into your love.

Breathe over me, and fan the embers of my desire into a flaming fire.
