## B. External checks

**1. Psalm 68:18, "received".** **CONFIRMED.** The Hebrew (68:19) reads לָקַחְתָּ מַתָּנוֹת בָּאָדָם, "you have received gifts among men". Brenton's Septuagint: "thou hast received gifts for man". The Greek text of the Septuagint itself would not open, so the Greek wording (*elabes domata*) is not confirmed from the page; the Douay-Rheims, from the Vulgate, also has "received". The sentence stands.

The other tradition is **confirmed** too. The Targum reads "You ascended to the firmament, Moses the prophet… gave gifts to humans" (Aramaic יְהַבְתָּא). The Peshitta in English: "you have given gifts to the children of men" (Bauscher); "thou hast blessed men with gifts" (Lamsa). The Syriac itself was not opened. Note that the Targum applies the verse to Moses.

**EDITORIAL, for Norm:** whether to add a sentence saying Paul was not alone in reading "gave". Not added.

**2. The summary of Psalm 68.** **Fair.** 68:16 "the mountain where God chooses to reign"; 68:17 the chariots, "from Sinai, into the sanctuary"; 68:18 "You have ascended on high. You have led away captives." "A long line of captives" and "the way a king came home from war" are the page's picture, and a reasonable one.

**3. *katartismos* / *katartizō*.** **CONFIRMED.** Ephesians 4:12 has καταρτισμόν, the noun, its only use in the New Testament. Mark 1:19 and Matthew 4:21 have καταρτίζοντας, the verb from the same root, of mending nets. "Getting something ready to do its job" fits the verb's use elsewhere, not only its etymology: Luke 6:40 (fully trained), Hebrews 10:5 (a body prepared), Hebrews 11:3 (the worlds framed), Hebrews 13:21 (equip you), Galatians 6:1 (restore).

**4. "Measure" three times.** **CONFIRMED.** μέτρον stands in 4:7, 4:13 and 4:16 (ἐν μέτρῳ), and nowhere else in 4:7–16.
