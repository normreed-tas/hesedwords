## What the sequence establishes

**1. The method is grammar first, encounter second — and it holds in four of five.**

Every piece opens inside the text's own mechanics before any application arrives. The
third-to-second-person lurch in 1:2. The doubled kiss, verb and its own cognate noun
piled on top. *Shem* and *shemen* leaned against each other so the name and the fragrance
are nearly the same word. *Mashak* as the word for a drawn bow, a hauled bucket, a body
pulled from a pit. *After* rather than *to*.

This is the most transferable thing the sequence has built, and it agrees with
COLLABORATION — analogy after the plain reading, never before.

The exception is the most recent piece. *Draw Me* opens at the Findon hearth and reaches
the verse second. It works, and the image is Norm's own rather than supplied, so it is
not the failure COLLABORATION warns about. But it is the one inversion in the set and it
is the last thing written before the pause. Worth knowing whether that was a deliberate
loosening or drift.

**2. Distance is already a working framework, not decoration.**

Three distinct spatial states are established across chapter 1, and each is named in the
prose rather than implied:

- **1:2 — interior nearness.** She is alone, he is elsewhere, and the gap closes by
  imagining. *The Song Begins in Longing* says it outright: the Song is choreographed by
  distance, this is the first of many such scenes, and here the distance folds at will
  because in the mind the beloved is only ever as far away as the next thought.
- **1:3 — the threshold.** Maidens outside drawn by what escapes the room; the Shulammite
  within. *Same oil, two distances from the jar.*
- **1:4 — motion behind.** *After*, not *to*. The request is explicitly not for arrival.
  Not a position but a path.

That is a real instrument, and chapter 2 is where it is tested — 2:9 puts him behind a
wall, at the windows, looking through the lattice, which is a fourth state and not
obviously reducible to any of the three. If the framework survives 2:9 it is sound. If it
has to be stretched, better to know at 2:9 than at 5:2.

**3. Bernard's position is settled and should not be relitigated.**

*The Song Begins in Longing* honours his close reading — the surplus in *of his mouth*,
the hidden manna that deepens the thirst it answers — and then refuses the husk-and-kernel
move on a stated ground: *da'at* runs continuously from the lovers' mouths up into the
knowledge of God with nothing along the line cancelled. *Draw Me! The Voice Matters* uses
him without resistance on *after, not to*.

So: take his reading, refuse his hierarchy of the body. Decided, and there is no need to
re-argue it at each passage. Name it where a passage turns on it; otherwise assume it.

**4. Read in order, the five are one argument, and the argument is about voice.**

Not chapter 1 exposited verse by verse. A single movement: longing spoken into the air
(1:2) → drawn by report, from outside (1:3) → petition, said to his face (1:4). The 1:4
article names the shape itself — report, address, petition, each costing more than the
last — and ends on *the voice really does matter*.

The sequence is self-aware about this. The 1:3 Selah ends by naming *draw me* as where it
wants to go next, and the next two pieces are both on that verse. The journey record
Norm describes is visible in the published order, not just asserted.

**5. What the Selahs do, and where they were heading.**

- **1:2** — fullest biography. 1979, the Navy, Papua New Guinea, the day the heart
  stopped. Closes on two questions.
- **1:3** — almost no biography, most exposure. *I love him the way the maidens do.* No
  anecdote at all; a position statement. Closes on prayer rather than questions, which
  CONVENTIONS permits when the piece has moved toward petition.
- **1:4** — shortest. Closes on two questions that are self-accusing rather than
  inviting: *have I asked to be drawn, or only ever voiced the wanting?*

Trajectory: less biography, more exposure, shorter. That is the direction the brief wants
anyway, so it is a habit to keep rather than correct.

## What it left open

**1. Half of 1:4 has never been read.**

*Draw Me! The Voice Matters* stops deliberately and says so: what the asking becomes —
the running, the shared joy, the chambers of the king — belongs to what comes next. It
never came next. *We will run. The king has brought me into his chambers. We will exult
and rejoice in you.*

This is the largest open item in chapter 1, and it carries a debt. The 1:3 reflection
built its central image — threshold against chamber — out of a word it borrowed from 1:4b
before 1:4b had been read. The chamber is currently doing metaphorical work on credit.

**2. The plural is unexamined, and a fork was taken without being named.**

*Therefore virgins love you. We will run. We will exult. Daughters of Jerusalem.* There is
a crowd in chapter 1 from verse 3 onward and none of the five pieces asks who they are or
why the poem keeps them in the room.

The 1:3 reflection uses them as a foil: maidens at the threshold, Shulammite in the
chamber, the contrast carrying the whole Selah. That reading requires the maidens to be
the lesser case. The text does not obviously say so, and there is a standing alternative
— Spurgeon reads the same plural as contagion rather than contrast, one soul's longing
making others run too. `[verify]` — that is from the description on our own Spurgeon
resource page, not from Sermon 2794 itself.

Contrast or contagion is a real fork, it was settled by implication, and it will come back
at every plural in the book. Worth deciding on purpose.

**3. 1:5–6 is the unwritten hinge, and it is why chapter 1 is not finished.**

*I am dark and beautiful... do not gaze at me... my mother's sons were angry with me; they
made me keeper of the vineyards, but my own vineyard I have not kept.*

First self-description. First defensiveness. First other people with power over her. First
admission of something not done. Everything before it is desire; this is the first cost of
it. Structurally it is the natural sequel to *draw me* — being drawn runs straight into
what she actually is — and it is where the spatial framework acquires a second party who
is neither lover nor chorus.

**4. The superscription has never been read.**

Because the first two pieces number the kiss as 1:1, the actual 1:1 — *The Song of Songs,
which is Solomon's* — has been passed over entirely. The superlative construction, the
Solomon attribution and what a book is claiming when it titles itself that way are all
untouched. That is a piece, and it is sitting in the gap the numbering created.

**5. One place where the standing caution on allegory was not kept.**

*Draw Me! The Voice Matters* builds *mashak* out to Jeremiah 31:3 and Hosea 11:4 — God
drawing Israel with *hesed* — which is solid Hebrew ground. Then: *it's the same word
Jesus used... Follow me.* As written that reads as the same word, and it is not; the
Gospel is Greek and the verb is different. The paragraph crosses from Hebrew lexis to
Gospel call without marking the join, and carries no warrant mark.

There is a sound version of the claim, and it is better than the loose one. The link is
not *mashak* but the *after* idiom — *halak acharei*, to walk after, which Deuteronomy
uses for covenant loyalty and Elijah uses on Carmel. The Greek of Mark 1:17 is *deute
opisō mou*, come after me. If the LXX renders *acharei* with *opisō* in those places, then
*draw me after you* and *come after me* share the idiom the article is actually reaching
for, and the claim becomes established rather than decorative.

`[verify]` — LXX of Deuteronomy 13:4 and 1 Kings 18:21 against Mark 1:17. I have not
opened them.

This is recorded as a repair available for future work, not a request to revise a
published page.

**6. Norm's current position is not recorded.**

His message setting up this note carries an unfilled bracket where his own answer was to
go — where he stands now, two months on from *I love him the way the maidens do*. It has
not been answered and I will not supply it. The threshold description is on the record as
of 30 June and nothing since supersedes it.

This matters more than it looks. The 1:3 Selah made a stated direction, the next two
pieces followed it, and then the work stopped for seven weeks. Whether the threshold moved
in that time is the thing that decides what the next Selah can honestly say.

## Considered and declined

**Opening chapter 2 now.** Declined. Three things in chapter 1 are unread — the second
half of 1:4, the whole of 1:5–6, and the superscription — and 1:5–6 is the passage the
journey was actually walking toward when it stopped. Starting chapter 2 would strand all
three and break the one continuous argument the five pieces have built. Chapter 2 is not
going anywhere.

**A standalone 1:3 article.** Declined. I flagged one as missing earlier in the session
and was wrong: *Draw Me! The Voice Matters* is indexed at 1:2–4 and takes 1:3 in properly.
The gap was inferred from a flat file listing rather than checked against the index.
Recorded so it is not re-raised.

## Loose ends

- **Versification.** Unresolved, and now visible in `SITE-INDEX-BRIEF.md` itself, which
  carries both schemes: the kiss at 1:1 for two pieces, and 1:2–4 for the article that
  includes the same verse. Every new piece has to choose. Recommendation remains standard
  ESV numbering, since ESV is the quotation text — which moves the kiss to 1:2 and leaves
  1:3 and 1:4 where they already sit.
- **Article-or-reflection first.** Five articles share the index date 2026-06-01,
  including *The Song Begins in Longing*, and its meta block gives a month with no day
  where later pieces give a full date. That looks like a backfill rather than a
  publication date. `[verify]` with Code from git history. It matters: the 1:1 article and
  reflection share their two closing questions verbatim and the article's penultimate
  paragraph is the reflection's Selah rewritten, so one was made from the other, and which
  way round tells us whether the article grew out of the journey or the journey was carved
  out of the article.
- **One closing question or two.** COLLABORATION says one, and only where there is a real
  fork. All three Song reflections close on two. Either this book has earned an exception
  or a drift is about to be copied forward.
- **Glossary.** `mashak`, `hesed`, `nashaq`, `shem` and `daat` are all live headwords;
  only the first two are linked from any Song piece. Note the slug is `daat`, not `da'at`,
  which the prose uses. `shemen` is not a headword, and the *shem*/*shemen* pairing in 1:3
  may argue for one.
- **Glossary link markup.** Existing Song pieces use
  `<a href="/glossary.html#mashak"><span class="heb">Mashak</span></a>`; Code gives the
  form as `<a class="gloss" href="/glossary.html#hesed"><em>hesed</em></a>`. Different
  class, different inner element. `CONVENTIONS.md` is the authority and I have not read it
  — resolve there before any page, not here.
- **Bernard.** *Draw Me! The Voice Matters* credits him with the *to* versus *after*
  distinction. `[verify]` against Sermon XXI itself; I have only the description on our own
  resource page. Separately, the three editorial flags sitting in
  `resources/bernard-song-of-songs.html` as HTML comments — Sermons XVIII, XLII, LXXXII —
  still await a check against a print copy.
- **The spatial framework has no file.** It has only ever lived in chat, and the account
  transition ended that. It is reconstructible from the published prose and the three
  states above are most of it. It needs somewhere to live, and everything in this repo is
  public, which is a consideration for working notes about where Norm is spiritually.
- **2:9 is the test case** for whether the distance framework generalises. Recorded here
  so it is not discovered mid-draft.

## Checked repo-side — Claude Code, 16 Sep 2026

Four of the items above are answerable from the repo. Answered here rather than left
open; the rest stand as written.

**Article-or-reflection first — settled, and the premise was wrong.**
Both 1:1 pieces were added in the **same commit**: `2f22994`, 25 June 2026, message
*"Publish The Song Begins in Longing and Let Him Kiss Me"*. Five files, one push. So
neither was made from the other across an interval — they shipped together, and git
cannot say which was drafted first because nothing distinguishes them in history.

The 2026-06-01 date is not a backfill artefact either. It is policy: `js/articles-data.js`
carries the note *"Article dates have no exact day recorded on their cards, so month-level
dates use the 1st as a placeholder."* Five articles share that date for exactly that
reason, and three of them have nothing to do with the Song. The inference that the date
looked artificial was right; the conclusion drawn from it was not available.

**Glossary link markup — CONVENTIONS is right and the Song pieces are the outliers.**
Site-wide: **55 pages** use `<a class="gloss" href="/glossary.html#slug"><em>word</em></a>`;
**3** use the bare form — `articles/draw-me-the-voice-matters.html`,
`reflections/understanding.html`, `word-studies/tzedaqah.html`. The `.gloss` class is
styled in `tokens.css`; the bare form gets no styling at all, so those three render their
glossary links as plain body links. Use the `class="gloss"` form in all new work. The
three existing pages are a cleanup item, not a pattern.

**The slug is both.** `#daat` resolves (id `seed_daat`) and so does `#da'at` (it matches
on the transliteration field, which is stored as `da'at`). `#yada` resolves separately to
`seed_yada`. So prose using *da'at* can link `#da'at` safely — the caution is unnecessary,
though `#daat` is the tidier form.

**`shemen` is not a headword** — confirmed. The string appears three times in
`glossary.html`, all inside another entry's notes, with no `seed_shemen` id. So a
*shem*/*shemen* piece would need one seeded first.

Still open and genuinely outside the repo: the Spurgeon sermon, the Bernard sermon, the
LXX check, and — the one that matters — Norm's own answer on where he now stands.
