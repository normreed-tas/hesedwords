## 1. The acrostic brings hesed to the front twice

Verse 64 closes the Ḥet stanza and opens with חַסְדְּךָ: "Your hesed, YHWH, fills the
earth." Verse 88 closes the Kaf stanza and opens with כְּחַסְדְּךָ: "According to your
hesed, give me life." These are the only two verses where hesed is the first word, and in
both cases the stanza's letter required a word starting that way.

- **Established:** the placement. Both verses confirmed, and no other verse opens with hesed.
- **Suggestive–thin:** any meaning in it. The plain explanation is that the poet needed a Ḥet
  word and then a Kaf word, and hesed was close to hand. Test that before reading more into it.

## 2. Verse 64 echoes Psalm 33:5

Psalm 33:5: "He loves tzedaqah and mishpat; the earth is full of the hesed of YHWH." Psalm
119:64 uses the same words, "the earth is full" and "hesed," and follows them with "teach me
your statutes."

- **Established:** the shared wording — four words in the same order. See the foot for the
  one difference, which matters.
- **Reading-within-frame:** in 33:5, hesed sits beside tzedaqah and mishpat. In 119:64 it is
  followed by a request to be taught, which is da'at. The same "earth full of hesed" line
  points to justice in one psalm and to knowing in the other.

## 3. "Let it come to me": verses 41 and 77

- v. 41: "Let your hesed come to me" (וִיבֹאֻנִי)
- v. 77: "Let your mercies (*rachamim*) come to me" (יְבֹאוּנִי)

It's the same verb (*bo'*), in the same form, spelled slightly differently. In v. 41 it's
hesed that comes, and in v. 77 it's mercies.

- **Established:** the shared verb and form.
- **Suggestive–thin:** whether the psalm treats hesed and rachamim as a pair.

## 4. Mercy and justice paired twice

- v. 149: "according to your hesed … according to your mishpat give me life"
- v. 156: "Great are your mercies (*rachamim*), YHWH; according to your mishpatim give me life"

Both times, a mercy word stands beside a justice word, with "give me life" as the request.

- **Established:** both verses.
- **Reading-within-frame:** the psalm never sets mercy against justice. It asks by both. The
  reflection used v. 149; v. 156 is a second witness that wasn't used.
- Note from the checks: in both verses it is the **justice** word that governs "give me
  life." In 149 hesed goes with "hear my voice"; in 156 rachamim stands alone as a statement.
  The reflection was corrected on exactly this point before it went up.

## 5. Where hesed is absent

Hesed's first appearance is v. 41, in the Vav stanza, and its last is v. 159, in the Resh
stanza. It doesn't appear in vv. 1–40 or vv. 160–176. (The drafting conversation said "the
last 16 verses"; it's 17, from v. 160 on.)

- **Established:** the distribution.
- **Suggestive–thin:** any meaning. The likeliest explanation is chance across 176 verses.
  Leave it unless something else points the same way.

## Loose ends

- Psalm 119 sits beside Psalm 89 (hesed 7 times) and Psalm 136 (26 times) in how often hesed
  appears, but nowhere near them in how central it is. That contrast could make a short piece
  on how often a word appears versus how much weight it carries. Counts confirmed — see the foot.
- *Did the Word Become a List?* (reflection, 23 June 2026) was considered as a companion and
  not linked. If the list/law theme comes back, the two pieces belong together.
- Lesson from the checks: quotations written from memory dropped "O LORD" in two places, and
  one count leaned further than the text allowed (v. 149). Check ESV wording against the text
  before build, not after.

## Verification — 4 October 2026 (Claude Code)

Checked against STEPBible TAHOT, Strong's-tagged with prefixes separated.

**1. Hesed as the first word.** Searching the opening word of all 176 verses for hesed
(H2617A) returns exactly two: **v. 64** חַסְדְּךָ and **v. 88** כְּחַסְדְּךָ. Both are the
eighth and last verse of their stanza.

**2. Psalm 33:5 and 119:64, letter by letter.**

```
Ps 33:5    אֹהֵב צְדָקָה וּמִשְׁפָּט   חֶסֶד   יְהוָה מָלְאָה הָאָרֶץ
Ps 119:64                         חַסְדְּךָ יְהוָה מָלְאָה הָאָרֶץ   חֻקֶּיךָ לַמְּדֵנִי
```

Four lemmas shared, in the same order: hesed, YHWH, *male'* (to fill), *erets* (earth).
**The one difference is the person.** 33:5 has חֶסֶד יְהוָה, *the hesed of YHWH*: the
psalmist speaking **about** God. 119:64 has חַסְדְּךָ יְהוָה, *your hesed, YHWH*: speaking
**to** him. That is the reflection's own observation — every hesed in Psalm 119 is "your
hesed", addressed to God — and it shows in the one line the two psalms share.

**3. The verb at vv. 41 and 77.** Same lemma, *bo'* (H0935G), same form: imperfect, 3rd
plural, with a 1st-singular object suffix — *let them come to me*. The spelling differs for
two reasons only. v. 41 carries the conjunction וְ, *and*, and writes the *u* vowel short
(וִיבֹאֻנִי); v. 77 has no conjunction and writes it full (יְבֹאוּנִי).

**4. v. 156.** רַחֲמֶיךָ רַבִּים יְהוָה כְּמִשְׁפָּטֶיךָ חַיֵּנִי — *your mercies are great,
YHWH; according to your judgments give me life.* Confirmed as written.

**5. Distribution.** Hesed's seven occurrences are vv. 41, 64, 76, 88, 124, 149, 159. None
before 41; none after 159. Seventeen verses follow v. 159.

**6. Psalms 89 and 136.** Hesed (H2617A): **7** in Psalm 89, **26** in Psalm 136. Both
confirmed.
