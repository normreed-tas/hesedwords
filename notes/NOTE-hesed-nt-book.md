# Hesed in the New Testament — standing project note

**Status: live.** Running through the rest of 2026. Purpose: so any session opening this
project knows the book exists and knows which pieces belong to it.

Established 22 Aug 2026 (conversation *Hesed embodied in Jesus*). Working note with the
full frame, audit and ledger is `hesed-nt-frame-NOTE.md`. This file is the shorter
standing version for the context set.

## What it is

A book built **from** standalone articles — not a book made **of** them.

Two things running at once, and the distinction matters at drafting time:

**The articles.** Each piece stands at its own URL on hesedwords.com and does its own work
for a reader who arrives at it cold. The sequence is real: a reader taking them in order
receives the argument. Norm's phrase for the form is *songs for the road*. No piece ever
waits on the book to be finished before it can be published.

**The book.** The finished book will have chapters. They will be based on the articles,
and the articles will form a large part of the material — but a chapter is not an article
promoted, and an article is not a chapter extracted. A chapter may draw on more than one
article, may need material written for it that no article carries, and can assume what an
earlier chapter established, which a standalone article never can.

What was set aside on 22 Aug was the idea that the published articles would be the
chapters, one for one. That still stands. Chapter structure is a later act of assembly,
not a constraint on what gets written now.

## The thesis in one line

In the Old Testament the search was for a word. In the New Testament it is for a person.
Same search, different object.

Supporting: *hesed* has no single Greek equivalent, so the New Testament distributes the
freight — *eleos* for the mercy, *charis* for the freeness, *pistis* for the faithfulness,
*agape* for the love, and *dikaiosynē* for what it does when challenged. A reader who only
knows the English never sees that these belong together.

## The frame

Norm's own sentence governs the shape: *it's a frame that will become evident.*

Sections are not pre-assigned. A grid built on John's prologue headings, with English
translation words as section titles, was considered and deliberately rejected — twenty
slots becomes a checklist, and a checklist sends the reader to the text looking for a slot
to fill. Sections emerge from the pieces once there are enough of them.

Publication order versus argument order is still open.

## The three disciplines

From the audit — name these before a reader does.

1. **Covenant shape.** *Hesed* presupposes a bond already in place. Much of what Jesus does
   runs outside it — the Syrophoenician woman, the centurion, the Samaritans. The answer is
   that he extends the covenant, but it must be said, not assumed.
2. **Character, not essence.** "God has always been *hesed*" describes how he acts and
   self-describes. It is not the same claim as "God is love," which is a statement about
   being. Sliding between the two puts a load on the frame it was not built for.
3. **Solvent risk.** The one to watch hardest, and Norm caught it himself in *The Treasure*
   with *kavod*. *Hesed* is one true thing tracked through the New Testament, not the key to
   it. The moment every passage is about *hesed*, the frame has stopped being evidence. A
   piece should only reach for *hesed* when the text hands it over.

## The theological gain

It answers the charge that the God of the Old Testament and the God of the New are
different characters. If Jesus is Exodus 34 walking about, they are the same one, and the
New Testament is not a change of temperament.

## Pieces in the book so far

**1. `Fitting` — Matthew 3, published.** `/articles/fitting.html`
The first piece written on the New Testament. Jesus' first recorded adult words as a
concession rather than a claim — *it is fitting for us*. Carries the four-Greek-words
material and the *tzedaqah* → *dikaiosynē* crossing. Functions as the opening.

**2. `Seeking` — Matthew 5–6, published.** `/articles/seeking.html`
Righteousness across the Sermon on the Mount — 5:6, 5:10, 5:20, 6:1, 6:33 as a spine
rather than a motif, spoken to a second person plural defined by persecution at 5:11 and
never redefined. Norm's thesis sentence: *seeking is not to attain to it but rather to
step into it.* Runs on *tzedaqah*, not on *hesed* — see discipline 3. Sits directly after
*Fitting* in sequence: same word, now practised under pressure.

Companion reflection: `You` (`/reflections/blessed-are-you.html`), Matthew 5. The
grammatical finding — eight Beatitudes say *those*, verse eleven says *you*, and it never
goes back. Selah is Saigon, 1990s. The reflection is where the observation was made; the
article is where it is argued.

**3. `Trampled` — Matthew 7:6 and Matthew 8, published.** `/articles/trampled.html`
The first piece in the sequence to run on *hesed* itself rather than on *tzedaqah*.
Assumes chapters five and six, so it sits after the Sermon article — a reader arriving cold
needs that piece to exist.

*Argument.* 7:6 gives a real danger and no category to apply, and Matthew 8 is what closes
off the shortcut. Built in four parts: a verse with no category / one word, twice / what
the pearl is / who he went to next.

*Established.* καταπατέω (Strong's G2662) appears twice in Matthew — 5:13 and 7:6 — and
nowhere else; five times in the New Testament (Matt 5:13, 7:6, Luke 8:5, Luke 12:1, Heb
10:29). Confirmed personally by Norm, 28 Aug 2026. Not outstanding.

*Established.* The salt saying is not about salt's value — what is trampled is salt that
has stopped being salt. This is the hinge, and it holds without interpretation.

*Established.* Matthew 8 goes to the leper (8:1–4), the centurion (8:5–13) and the men
among the tombs in Gentile territory (8:28–34). The centurion is the load-bearing one,
because Jesus does not merely receive him — *with no one in Israel have I found such faith*
measures Israel against him. Reversal, not exception.

*Reading-within-frame.* That the pearl is *hesed*. Matthew 13 never names the pearl; the
point of comparison is the transaction. The article concedes this openly on the page — the
word is mine, not Matthew's. Norm's reading, and better than "the pearl is Jesus," which
fights the parable: the merchant sells everything for the pearl, so the pearl cannot be the
one who paid. Norm has thought and preached the Jesus reading, and says so in the article.

*Reading-within-frame.* That the asking of 7:7–11 is for discernment. Position, not
vocabulary — 7:11 says *good things*. The article states it cannot prove this.

*Suggestive.* Hebrews 10:29, *trampled underfoot the Son of God* — same verb, said of
someone inside the covenant. Footnoted, not argued.

*Suggestive.* The Gadarene herd on the hillside. Setting, not a category of person;
footnoted as arrangement rather than argument.

*Left out deliberately.* The narrow gate, the fruit test, the *Lord, Lord* men, the house
on rock, the storm, the two would-be followers. The article says so on the page.

*Not in this piece.* *Hesed* as the one thing that can only be trampled while it is being
extended, because extending it is the only way it exists — a doctrine keeps on a shelf and
this does not. This was the thesis first reached for on 28 Aug and it did not survive
drafting: the article argues the missing category instead, which is what the text actually
yields. The formulation is good and is held here for a later piece.

*No Selah.* Deliberate. *So I Ask* already holds the personal weight, and reaching for the
same moment would make the article read as the reflection with footnotes.

Companion reflection: `So I Ask` (`/reflections/so-i-ask.html`), Matthew 7:6. Where the
observation was made. Selah is a man in prison and a question left open. One companion line
at the foot of each piece, carrying both directions.

*Held, not yet placed — Matthew 8 as answer to the establishment's verdict.* Norm's
observation, 28 Aug 2026: chapter 8 goes to precisely the people the establishment would
have filed under *dog* and *pig*. This is in the article. What is not in it, and is held:
that this does not answer whether to give — it rules out the shortcut of taking the
establishment's verdict as the answer. Kept out of the reflection because it would resolve
a Selah that must not resolve. (A related but weaker observation was recorded here first in
error: that the dangerous figures in the rest of ch. 7 are insiders — false prophets in
sheep's clothing, the *Lord, Lord* men with credentials and results. Still true and possibly
usable, but a second point, not this one.)

## Verified source material held for this book

**Moravians, St Thomas, 1732 — verified.** Dober and Nitschmann sailed from Copenhagen
8 Oct 1732, reached St Thomas 13 Dec 1732. They did **not** sell themselves into slavery —
white slavery was not permitted in the islands, so the transaction was unavailable. They
were willing and were refused, then went as free missionaries and worked with their hands.
Nitschmann: *"I am a carpenter, and will ply my trade."* Dober a potter, and back in Germany
by 1734. The motto *"may the Lamb that was slain receive the reward of his suffering"* is
genuinely Moravian but has no primary attestation to that dock on that day — use as motto,
not as quotation. The verified version is the better one: the legend is an attainment story,
the fact is a stepping-in story.

**Gandhi — verified, seven items.**

- *"I like your Christ, I do not like your Christians"* — not attributable, do not use.
  Traces to a 1927 *Harvard Crimson* paraphrase; likely originates with Bara Dada in
  E. Stanley Jones, *The Christ of the Indian Road* (1925), p. 114.
- *"If Christians would really live according to the teaching of Christ…"* — same verdict,
  no primary attestation.
- Church exclusion is real but Durban 1914, hearing C.F. Andrews — two decades after the
  London reading and after satyagraha was formed. It is not the cause of his not converting;
  his stated reasons are different. The order matters: he was inside the text already when
  the church shut the door.
- *"the New Testament produced a different impression, especially the Sermon on the Mount
  which went straight to my heart"* — genuine. The verses *"delighted me beyond measure."*
- He quotes Matthew 5:39–40 only — resist not evil, the other cheek, the coat and cloak.
  **Not** 5:41, the second mile.
- He does not subordinate the Sermon to the Gita: *"competes almost on equal terms with the
  Bhagavad Gita for the domination of my heart"* (CWMG 13:220); and if deprived of the Gita
  he would derive the same joy from the Sermon (*Young India*, 31 Dec 1931). *"It was the
  New Testament which really awakened me to the rightness and value of passive resistance"*
  (Doke). The honest ceiling is *competes almost on equal terms* — he is not saying the
  Sermon wins.
- Tolstoy (*The Kingdom of God Is Within You*) is load-bearing but came after the London
  reading — it deepened a reading already made rather than supplying it.

**Vanessa Goodwin / AIC paper** — held for the Exodus 34 piece. Two `[verify]` marks
outstanding: exact details of her death, and the superlative *"single biggest predictor,"*
which overstates the paper's finding.

## Candidate pieces not yet written

- **Exodus 34 and the third and fourth generation** — the clause read as a limit on the
  consequence rather than a threat. The limit is the mercy. Vanessa Goodwin and the six
  Tasmanian families are the evidence. Has a Selah attached, which is why it is likely the
  collection's centre of gravity.
- **Genesis 15 and the cross** — the cost accepted in the dark while Abram slept.
  Galatians 3:13 supplies the covenant frame. Norm's position: the cross sits inside *hesed*
  from the beginning.
- **John 1** — what Exodus 34 gives as a proclaimed name, John gives as a man. Live fork
  held: John's pair at 1:14 is *charis kai aletheia*, not *eleos* — if *hesed we'emet* is
  behind it, John has quietly declined the LXX's usual rendering for a better one. Footnote
  or its own beat, undecided.
- **Jehoshaphat, 2 Chronicles 20** — the choir sends *ki le'olam ḥasdo* ahead of the army;
  the character of God as the thing marched behind. The NT question in reverse: what does it
  look like when the thing you march behind has a face? Possible gateway piece, possible
  piece of its own.
- **The Sermon as instructions for a people under pressure** — arising 26 Aug 2026. Note the
  overlap: `/reflections/love-your-enemies.html` already holds the second mile, the
  occupation and the detention-camp commandant. Any piece here must know what it is adding.
