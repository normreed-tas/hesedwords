## A1. *yaqar* in the Psalter — FAILED

There are nine occurrences across the three lemmas. All of them are listed here.

| Ref (Eng) | Lemma | Text | Who values | What is valued |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| 49:8 (Heb 9) | verb | the ransom of a life "is costly" | **No one.** A price stated as too high for any human to pay | the redemption price of a life |
| 72:14 | verb | "precious is their blood **in his sight**" | **The king.** vv. 12–14 are the king's acts; v. 1 "Give the king your justice, O God" | the blood of the poor |
| 139:17 | verb | "How precious **to me** are your thoughts, O God!" | **The psalmist** | **God's thoughts** |
| 36:7 (Heb 8) | adj. | "How precious is your steadfast love, O God!" | **The psalmist** | **God's *hesed*** |
| 37:20 | adj. | "like the **glory** of the pastures" | No one | the brief splendour of grass (BDB sense 3, "splendid") |
| 45:9 (Heb 10) | adj. | "among your **ladies of honour**" | Social esteem | women of the court |
| 116:15 | adj. | "Precious **in the sight of the LORD** is the death of his saints" | **God** | the death of his faithful |
| 49:12 (Heb 13) | noun | "man **in his pomp** will not remain" | Human honour | human standing |
| 49:20 (Heb 21) | noun | "man **in his pomp** yet without understanding" | Human honour | human standing |

**Tally:**

| Direction | Count | Where |
|---|---|---|
| God appraising | **1** | 116:15 |
| A human ruler appraising | **1** | 72:14, the king |
| **The psalmist appraising something of God's** | **2** | **36:7** (God's *hesed*) and **139:17** (God's thoughts) |
| No appraiser: price, splendour or social honour | **5** | 49:8, 37:20, 45:9, 49:12, 49:20 |

**What is true:**
- The pattern "God normally appraises; 36:7 reverses it" does not hold.
- God is the appraiser once in nine.
- The "reversal" is not unique: Ps 139:17 has the identical construction, the psalmist
  exclaiming how precious something of God's is ("How precious to me are your thoughts,
  O God!").

**What survives, and may be better.** *yaqar* in the Psalms is a word for **price and
rarity**. It is used:
- of a ransom no one can afford (49:8);
- of honour that does not last (49:12, 49:20);
- of splendour that burns off like grass (37:20).

Against that, two things are called precious and *do* last: God's *hesed* (36:7) and God's
thoughts (139:17). That contrast is checkable from the list above. The 49/37 "pomp" and
"glory" uses make a strong foil: what people prize perishes; what the psalmist prizes is
God's.

**72:14 (correction to the recalled instance).** It is still a good line, but the eye is the
king's. The usual Christian reading takes it as the messianic king. Don't present it as "in
God's sight".

## A2. Beyond the Psalter

Not pursued, since A1 failed.

One instance worth knowing, because it is the clear case of *God* appraising *people*:
**Isa 43:4**, "Because you are precious in my eyes, and honoured, and I love you." That is
the verb, 3365. Other verb uses are mostly human regard: 1 Sam 18:30, 26:21; 2 Kgs 1:13–14.

## A4. Lexical range — CONFIRMED

**BDB on 3368:** "precious, rare, splendid, weighty".
1. **Precious:**
   - (a) costly, of building stones and wealth: 1 Kgs 5:31, 7:9–11; Isa 28:16;
   - (b) highly valued, of a life, of wisdom, and of God's steadfast love: Prov 3:15;
     **Ps 36:8 (Heb)**; Ps 116:15;
   - (c) collectively, precious stones and jewels: 2 Sam 12:30; 1 Kgs 10:2, 10;
     Ezek 27:22.
2. **Rare:** 1 Sam 3:1, "the word of the LORD was rare in those days".
3. **Splendid:** Ps 37:20.
4. **Weighty:** a late usage, Eccl 10:1.

**Non-Psalter instances for the reflection:**
- 1 Kgs 10:2, the Queen of Sheba's "very much gold and **precious stones**";
- Prov 3:15, wisdom "is more **precious** than jewels".

It is a valuer's word, not a warm one.

HALOT not checked (not available).

## B1. Versification — CONFIRMED

Hebrew Ps 36 has **13** verses; v. 1 is the title ("To the choirmaster. Of David, the servant
of the LORD"). The English has 12. **Hebrew 36:8 = English 36:7**, and every English verse
number is one less than the Hebrew.

## B2. 36:1–2 and 36:7 — wording CONFIRMED; the v. 2 clause is NOT secure

**ESV wording:**
- 36:1: "Transgression speaks to the wicked deep in his heart; there is no fear of God before
  his eyes."
- **ESV footnote on v. 1:** "Some Hebrew manuscripts, Syriac, Jerome (compare Septuagint);
  most Hebrew manuscripts *in my heart*". The Masoretic text (WLC, checked) reads *libbi*,
  "my heart".
- 36:2: "For he flatters himself in his own eyes that his iniquity cannot be found out and
  hated." No ESV footnote.
- 36:7: "How precious is your steadfast love, O God! The children of mankind take refuge in
  the shadow of your wings."

**The Hebrew of 36:2 (Heb 3):** *ki heḥeliq elav be-einav limtso avono lisno*. Word for word:
"for he/it made-smooth to him in his eyes, to find his iniquity, to hate".

**Text-critical status: NOT SECURE.** Every commentary I opened calls it difficult:
- **Cambridge:** "A much disputed verse." It weighs three possible subjects for "flatters":
  the wicked man, **Transgression**, or God. It prefers **Transgression**.
- **Keil & Delitzsch:** take **Transgression**, personified, as the most probable subject.
  They note the language "look[s] like corruptions of the text" to modern readers, without
  declaring it corrupt.
- **Ellicott:** "A sentence of great difficulty". The Hebrew can be "he" or "it".
- **Pulpit:** "Another very obscure verse." **Barnes:** "considerable obscurity in the
  original."
- **JFB, Gill, Poole:** read it as the man's self-deception, as ESV does.

**For the reflection.** The "two appraisals" reading needs the man to be the one appraising
himself. That is the ESV reading and a respectable one, but it is not the only one.
- On the Transgression reading, sin is the flatterer: it makes things smooth "in his eyes".
- **"In his own eyes" is common to every reading.** So the safest form of the beat is about
  **whose eyes do the valuing**: the man's eyes, flattered, against the psalmist's verdict
  at v. 7. That stands whoever the subject of "flatters" is.
- Do not build on "he flatters himself" as a settled reading.

## B3. *chasah* and *hesed* — CONFIRMED, different roots

36:7b *yeḥesayun*, "take refuge", is lemma **2620** (חסה). *ḥasdekha* in the same verse is
**2617** (חסד). Different roots; the shared consonants are coincidence. The draft is right
not to link them.

## B4. *adanekha* and Eden — low priority

*ʿadanekha* (36:8 English, "your delights") is lemma **5730**, *ʿeden* "delight". The garden
of Eden is a separate lemma, **5731**. Same consonants. Many link them, but the tagging
treats them as two words, and I opened no lexicon on the point. **Too loose for the body; at
most "the word sounds like Eden".**

## B5. *meshokh* (36:10 English) and Song 1:4 — CONFIRMED, same lemma

Both are **4900**, *mashakh*, "draw, prolong":
- Ps 36:10, "**continue** (draw out) your steadfast love";
- Song 1:4, *mashkheni*, "**draw** me after you".

That is a cross-reference only, per the brief.
