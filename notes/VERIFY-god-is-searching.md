## A. Arithmetic

### A1. "Three years" of disease (ch. 11, ch. 18) — **HOLD**

The ESV (Bible Hub) dates the disease to the thirty-ninth year at 16:12 and Asa's death to the forty-first year at 16:13.
- **Counted inclusively** (39, 40, 41), that gives three years. Inclusive counting is ordinary in regnal reckoning.
- **As elapsed time**, it is about two years.
- Matthew Henry (Bible Hub, 16:1-14) says "Two years before he died."

So "three years" is defensible only on the inclusive count, and the counting is genuinely ambiguous.

Two lines are affected:
- ch. 11: "He lived with it for three years" and "It is the three years."
- ch. 18: "For three years his feet were in agony."

The verify file's own options are "two or three years" or dropping the number. Either is safe.

### A2. "Twenty-one years earlier" (ch. 10) — **arithmetic confirmed; see A4**

The order in the text runs like this:
- Azariah meets Asa on the return from Gerar (15:1-2, after 14:15).
- The assembly uses "the spoil that they had brought" (15:11).
- The assembly is dated to the third month of the fifteenth year (15:10).

So the battle, Azariah and the assembly all fall within the fifteenth year or just before it. The thirty-sixth year minus the fifteenth is 21, so twenty-one holds on the text's own numbers. A4 changes that.

### A3. "Twenty years" without war (ch. 5, ch. 7) — **arithmetic confirmed; see A4**

From the covenant in the fifteenth year (15:10) to "no more war until the thirty-fifth year" (15:19) is 20 years.

### A4. The thirty-sixth year — **HOLD. This one is load-bearing.**

The problem, from the ESV (Bible Hub):
- Baasha began to reign in Asa's third year and reigned twenty-four years (1 Kings 15:33).
- Elah son of Baasha began to reign in Asa's twenty-sixth year (1 Kings 16:8).

So Baasha was dead about ten years before Asa's thirty-sixth year, and 16:1 cannot stand as Asa's thirty-sixth year taken literally.

Separately, 1 Kings 15:16 says "there was war between Asa and Baasha king of Israel all their days." That sits awkwardly beside "no more war until the thirty-fifth year" (15:19).

The commentators, all opened on the Bible Hub page for 16:2:
- **Keil & Delitzsch:** the 35th and 36th are a copyist's error for the 15th and 16th, with ל (30) and י (10) interchanged.
- **JFB:** the count runs from the division of the kingdom, which makes it equal to Asa's 16th year.
- **Gill:** notes that Baasha was dead by then.

**What follows for the book.** Both main harmonisations put Ramah in Asa's sixteenth year, about one year after Azariah, not twenty-one. On either of them:
- the "twenty years of quiet" (ch. 5, ch. 7) disappears;
- "twenty-one years earlier" (ch. 10) disappears;
- "Problems of the same kind, twenty years apart" (ch. 12) disappears;
- "Twenty years earlier Asa had carried the sacred gifts" (ch. 6) disappears;
- "twenty-odd years later" (ch. 13) disappears.

Only a flat reading of 15:19 and 16:1 keeps the twenty years, and that flat reading cannot be squared with 1 Kings 16:8.

The book currently states the thirty-sixth and thirty-ninth years flat, and builds a twenty-year interval on them. **That is not tenable without at least a note. As things stand, the interval is the book's weakest factual joint.**

No choice between the scholars is made here. The decision on how the book handles the dating is Norm's. The options as Code sees them:
1. Keep the Chronicler's numbers as the Chronicler's, and say so in a note.
2. Soften the intervals to "years later" throughout.
3. Rebuild the chapters that lean on the interval.

### A5. "Three sentences" (ch. 3) — **confirmed**

ESV 14:11 (Bible Hub) has three sentences:
1. "O LORD, there is none like you to help, between the mighty and the weak."
2. "Help us, O LORD our God, for we rely on you, and in your name we have come against this multitude."
3. "O LORD, you are our God; let not man prevail against you."

The block quote in ch. 3 matches the ESV exactly.

## B. The seeking thread

### B1. Is it one verb? — **No. HOLD.**

The verb at each reference was read from the Strong's tagging on Bible Hub's Hebrew text pages:

| Ref | Verb | Form |
|---|---|---|
| 14:4 | *darash* (1875) | Qal infinitive, "to seek" |
| 15:2 | *darash* (1875) | Qal imperfect, "if you seek him" |
| 15:4 | ***baqash*** (1245) | Piel, "and sought him" |
| 15:12 | *darash* (1875) | Qal infinitive, "to seek" |
| 15:15 | ***baqash*** (1245) | Piel, "they had sought him" |
| 16:12 | *darash* (1875) | Qal perfect, "he did not seek" |

*Darash* also occurs at 14:7 (twice: "we have sought the LORD… we have sought him") and at 15:13 ("whoever would not seek").

So the thread is **mainly *darash***. It covers the command, Azariah's condition, the covenant, the death penalty, the building speech, and the final negation at 16:12. But **two links in it are *baqash***: 15:4 ("in their distress… sought him") and 15:15 ("sought him with their whole desire").

Chapter 11 currently says "That is the word the whole story has been built on." Its list includes 15:15, which is the other verb.

The *darash* thread on its own (14:4, 14:7, 15:2, 15:12, 15:13, 16:12) is strong and runs from start to finish. The claim survives if it is stated as *darash* and 15:15 is dropped from the list, or if it is stated as "seek" in English with the two verbs acknowledged.

### B2. 16:9 and the seeking verbs — **thematic only**

"Run to and fro" is *shut* (7751, Polel participle). "Show himself strong" is *chazaq* (2388, Hithpael infinitive). Neither is related to *darash* or *baqash*.

The book's move from people seeking God to God searching is therefore **thematic, not lexical**. Ch. 8 does not claim a word link ("God is the one searching"), so it stands as written. If the title or the back matter ever implies a shared word, it should be marked as thematic.

## C. 16:9

### C1. KJV wording — **confirmed exactly**

KJV (Bible Hub): "For the eyes of the LORD run to and fro throughout the whole earth, to shew himself strong in the behalf of them whose heart is perfect toward him."

### C2. "Show himself strong" and "give strong support" — **fair, with one nuance**

The verb is *chazaq* in the Hithpael, the infinitive לְהִתְחַזֵּק.

BDB (Bible Hub 2388) lists the Hithpael senses as:
1. strengthen oneself;
2. put forth strength;
3. withstand;
4. followed by עִם: "hold strongly with."

BDB places 16:9 under sense 4, with עִם לְבָבָם. That supports "strong support / strongly support", so **the book's "probably closer to the plain sense" is a fair statement of where the lexical weight lies.**

The nuance is that the split between versions is not old against new. From the Bible Hub parallel page:
- **Show himself strong:** KJV, and also the modern CSB and BSB.
- **Strongly support:** NASB.
- **Give strong support:** ESV.
- **Strengthen:** NIV and another modern version.

"The newer wording" in ch. 8 reads as the ESV's wording specifically, and the sentence is fine as it stands.

### C3. *Shalem* — **confirmed, with a correction to the glossary plan**

At 16:9 the word is the adjective *shalem* (8003), tagged "perfect, Adj-ms". BDB (Bible Hub) gives "complete, safe, at peace". Its sense 1 is "complete": full or perfect, as of a full weight or stones left whole. The Bible Hub note on 8003 renders the heart idiom "wholly devoted" and ties the word to the root of *shalom*.

The book's gloss, "whole, entire, all of a piece; not about flawlessness", is supported. "Complete / whole" is the lexical core. Moral flawlessness is not.

**On the glossary slug:** the site's only *shalem* entry is `seed_shalem`, at `glossary.html` line 350. It glosses the **verb** ("to complete, to make whole; to perform a vow"), which is Strong's 7999, from the same root. The book uses the **adjective** (8003). Linking to `seed_shalem` would point readers to the vow sense. **HOLD:** add a separate entry for the adjective, or don't link the word.

### C4. 15:17 and 16:9 — **confirmed: the same word**

Both verses have *shalem* (8003, adjective masculine singular), tagged on Bible Hub. Chapter 8's central difficulty stands.

## D. Spurgeon

All quotations were checked against the full sermon text on spurgeon.org: *A Lesson from the Life of King Asa*, MTP no. 1,152, vol. 20.

**Page numbers could not be checked.** spurgeon.org's PDF of the sermon (12 pages, consistent with pp. 25–36) is a scan with no text layer. Norm's scan remains the source for page numbers.

**Date.** spurgeon.org's page metadata labels 1 January 1874 as `datePreached`. A bare 1 January is the kind of value that is often a placeholder. The printed sermon carries no date, and the page shows no evidence of a preaching date. **Safest: cite it as *Metropolitan Tabernacle Pulpit*, vol. 20 (1874), no. 1,152, and give no day.**

### D1. "A bad man may do better than a good man on some one particular occasion." — **wording exact**

In Spurgeon it follows his remark that Rehoboam "did not put him in prison", and it continues "…and so Rehoboam did better in that matter than Asa did." The book's use matches his.

### D2. "It is very wrong to send for physicians in place of crying to God." — **wording exact**

Spurgeon's sentence is: "It is not wrong to send for physicians, it is quite right; but it is very wrong to send for physicians in place of crying to God, thus putting the human agency before the divine…" The book's framing ("there is nothing wrong with sending for a doctor") matches his first clause.

### D3. The Syria paragraph (ch. 9) — **HOLD. Misattributed in one respect.**

Spurgeon does say four of the things the paraphrase gives him:
- Ben-hadad "did not content himself with a battle or two, but he fell to plundering the Israelites and murdering them by wholesale";
- "the poor afflicted people had Asa to thank for it";
- "He who ought to have been a brother to the Israelites became their destroyer";
- "The beginning of sin is like the letting out of waters; none can foresee what devastation the floods may cause."

He does **not** say that Syria became "in later years the scourge of Israel". In the sermon he is describing **the very campaign Asa paid for**, Ben-hadad's attack in 16:4, and he refers to no later Syrian campaigns. The words "scourge" and "later years" do not occur anywhere in the sermon.

So the answer to "which later Syrian campaigns?" is: **none. Spurgeon does not go beyond 16:4.**

The verify file says to cut the paragraph if it cannot be substantiated. As written, it cannot. A corrected paraphrase is possible, but it would describe the 16:4 campaign rather than a later one, and so would not serve ch. 9's point about "from now on." Either way the decision is Norm's.

### D4. Rehoboam and Shemaiah — **confirmed**

ESV (Bible Hub):
- 11:4: "So they listened to the word of the LORD and returned and did not go against Jeroboam."
- 12:6: "Then the princes of Israel and the king humbled themselves and said, 'The LORD is righteous.'"

There is no imprisonment, and Spurgeon says the same.

## E. Geography and detail

### E1. Ijon, Dan and Abel-maim — **confirmed**

Easton's dictionary (biblestudytools.com) describes the three places like this:
- **Ijon:** "a town in the north of Palestine, belonging to the tribe of Naphtali", "in a fertile and beautiful little plain called Merj Ayun", "a few miles northwest of the site of Dan".
- **Abel-beth-maacah:** "in the neighbourhood of Dan and Ijon, in the tribe of Naphtali", about 6 miles west-north-west of Dan.
- **Abel-maim** is the Chronicler's name for the same place, according to Keil & Delitzsch.

"In the far north, up where the hills run on into Lebanon" is fair: Merj Ayun lies in present-day southern Lebanon.

### E2. "From Dan to Beersheba" — **confirmed**

ESV: Judges 20:1 has "from Dan to Beersheba", and 1 Samuel 3:20 has "all Israel from Dan to Beersheba".

### E3. "The store cities of Naphtali were granaries. They held the region's food." — **HOLD, minor**

"Store cities" (*miskenot*) are supply or depot cities. At 2 Chronicles 32:28 (ESV), Hezekiah's storehouses are "for the yield of grain, wine, and oil". Keil & Delitzsch, on 16:4, take the Chronicler's phrase as "the magazines or stores of the cities of Naphtali". They note that Kings has "all Kinneroth", the fertile district by Galilee, which "might be called the granary" of Naphtali.

"Granaries" is defensible as a figure. "They held the region's food" overstates it.

Suggested wording: "The store cities of Naphtali held its supplies — grain, wine and oil." This changes a claim slightly, so it comes back to Norm.

### E4. Naphtali and 15:9 — **confirmed**

ESV 15:9 names only "Ephraim, Manasseh, and Simeon". Naphtali is not among them, so the distinction ch. 6 draws is accurate.

### E5. "The Ethiopians and the Libyans" — **reported**

The Hebrew is *Kushim* and *Lubim*. Three commentaries on 16:8 (Bible Hub) handle it:
- **Cambridge:** the Lubim "are not mentioned in 2 Chronicles 14:9-13, but as they were auxiliaries of the Egyptians (2 Chronicles 12:3) it is quite probable that they represent the help given by Egypt to the Cushites."
- **Ellicott:** takes 16:8 simply as a reference back to the Zerah battle.
- **Benson:** Libyans in Africa, or a people descended from them.

ESV 12:3 does list "Libyans, Sukkiim, and Ethiopians" in Shishak's army. No commentator opened sees a contradiction. Quoting the verse without comment is defensible.
