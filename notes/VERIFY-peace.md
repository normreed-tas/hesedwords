# VERIFY — *Peace* (article, Ephesians 2:11–22)

Answers `peace.verify.md` (17 November 2026). Checked by Claude Code, 17 November 2026. Sources: WEB on Bible Hub, verse by verse; SBLGNT; Wikipedia, "Temple Warning inscription" and "Ara Pacis".

| Item | Result |
|---|---|
| WEB wording: Eph 2:11–21; 6:12, 6:15, 6:20, 6:23 | **CONFIRMED**, with one comma fixed at 2:19 (WEB: "with the saints and of the household of God"). |
| Acts 21:29, 21:30–32, 28:16 | **CONFIRMED**: "Trophimus the Ephesian"; "they supposed that Paul had brought him into the temple"; seized, dragged out, "trying to kill him", soldiers; "the soldier who guarded him". Acts 21:27 has "the Jews from Asia". |
| 1. Ara Pacis | **CONFIRMED**: "commissioned by the Roman Senate on July 4, 13 BC to honour the return of Augustus". |
| 2. Notices in Greek and Latin | **CONFIRMED**: "Both Greek and Latin inscriptions on the temple's balustrade". The two surviving tablets are in Greek. |
| 3. Two found | **CONFIRMED**: "Two of these tablets have been found", one complete (1871) and one fragment (1936). |
| 4. Wording | **CONFIRMED** as a fair paraphrase: "Whoever is caught will be himself responsible for his ensuing death." |
| 5. *dogmata* | **REWORDED (Norm).** The word means official decrees: Luke 2:1 (Caesar's decree), Acts 17:7 ("the decrees of Caesar"), Acts 16:4, Col 2:14, Eph 2:15. Those are its five NT uses. "Posted publicly" is not part of the word's sense. It now reads: "It means decrees, the rulings of an authority, the kind that were often posted up for the public to read." |
| 6. Roman imprisonment | **Left as written (Norm).** It is the traditional view. |
