- `word-studies/is-wicked-a-verdict-or-a-character.html` — *rasha'*
- `articles/what-did-jesus-call-wicked.html` — Psalm 58, Psalm 82, Matthew 23

## How they were made

**The starting point.** Not a topic and not a plan. A morning's reading and an objection:
Psalm 58 ends with the righteous bathing their feet in blood, and that appeared to
contradict everything the prison work is built on. The opening question was not *help me
write about Psalm 58* but closer to *what have I got wrong here* — and the assumption that
something was wrong, rather than that the text was defective, is what made the rest
possible. The exploratory phase ran for several exchanges before any writing was proposed.

**The split into three.** The material would not fit one piece. Three distinct things were
present: a personal reversal (having filed the men in the cells under *wicked* without
noticing), a lexical finding (*rasha'* is forensic before it is moral), and a canonical case
(Psalm 82, Jesus's usage, Matthew 23).

Each was allocated to one piece and **material was not allowed to migrate**. When the word
study took *rasha'*, the article's planned lexical beat was cut rather than duplicated. That
left the article shorter and better.

**The reflection was written first, deliberately** — its power depends on the framework not
yet existing. Writing the article first would have contaminated it.

## Decisions log

| Decision | Reasoning |
|---|---|
| Reflection first | The reversal had to be written while still fresh; the article would have supplied answers the reflection needs to lack. |
| Composite *Selah*, not one man | The experience is genuinely repeated; forcing it into a single occasion would have been a fiction. Owned as composite in the opening line. |
| Second person in the *Selah* | Puts the reader in the seat rather than describing the seat. |
| Grandchildren opening kept in two pieces | Deliberate echo across companion pieces rather than an accident. |
| Bracton cut from the reflection, kept in the article | The idea survives in the reflection as *a debt nobody thought they were carrying*; the citation belongs where it can be done properly. |
| Bonhoeffer added late | Discovered during fact-checking. His objection was a live challenge the article was silent on; silence would have been a weakness a reader could find. |
| Article closes on abstraction | Ordinarily the close lands on a person. Permitted here because the reflection carries the concrete ending for the pair. |

## Editorial interventions — before and after

Three sentence-level interventions, all of the same kind: register climbing, abstract nouns
doing work that verbs should do.

**1.** *Before:* "…if it is wrong, then what has been discarded is not a primitive impulse
that the gospel outgrew."
*After:* "If it is wrong, we have not set aside something the gospel improved on. We have set
aside a text that says what the gospel says."
Author's note: *"it is more you than me."* The phrase *primitive impulse that the gospel
outgrew* is reaching for elegance and paying for it with distance.

**2.** *Before:* "The parallels with Psalm 58 are not thematic resemblance."
*After:* "The two psalms are not merely similar. They do the same thing, in the same order."
Abstract summarising noun replaced with verbs the reader can check against the text.

**3.** *Before:* "Which leaves the imprecatory psalms doing something other than what they
are usually accused of."
*After:* "Which leaves the imprecatory psalms — the ones that call down harm, that ask God to
break and scatter and undo — doing something other than what they are usually accused of."
Author's instruction: *define for a twelve-year-old but keep the word.* The definition sits
inside the dashes; nothing stops to teach.

## The two failures worth recording

**The invented person.** A draft closing beat introduced a young man with twenty-five
placements who asked after his sister at every visit. It was fabricated to satisfy the
convention that a reflection closes on a person. The author's response: *"Not the right
ending and it's not true."*

The convention is real. Inventing material to satisfy it is not permitted. **Where the
convention and the truth conflict, the convention gives way.**

**The wrong ending.** The same draft closed on helplessness — *I have no power to change
that… the rest I hand over.* The author's correction: the reflection is about failing to
**name** something, not about being unable to fix it. The psalm hands over words, not
resignation.

That correction is the hinge of the whole set. Everything downstream — the Amos close, the
article's argument that imprecation is speech rather than passivity, the Bonhoeffer
qualification — follows from it. It came from the author, not the collaboration.

## The cut

The near-final draft ran the Bracton passage — *not granted, owed* — straight into *Twenty-five
placements. Twenty-five rooms. And a debt nobody thought they were carrying.* Two hammer-blows
in consecutive paragraphs. The author's decision, after seeing it in context: *"take it out."*
Then, on reflection, the Bracton passage went instead and the rooms came back.

Standing principle confirmed: **stop earlier than feels safe**, and judge it on the page
rather than in the plan.

## Warrant discipline in practice

- Psalm 58 / Psalm 82 pairing — **established**
- The judicial address in Psalm 58:1 — **established**
- Forensic sense of *rasha'* in legal texts — **established**
- *Rasha'* as extended forensic rather than developed moral sense — **reading-within-frame**,
  marked in the prose by *I think*
- Jesus deliberately invoking the Psalm 82 tradition at John 10:34 — **reading-within-frame**,
  softened to *he knew the tradition*
- Bracton's mercy of compassion as *hesed* — **reading-within-frame**, written as *hesed in a
  law book* rather than an identification
- The two servant parables as evidence Jesus defined wickedness as perverted justice —
  **reading-within-frame**; the parables are not about judges, and the unifying category is
  supplied

## Fact-checking, and what it caught

Three claims were flagged during drafting as needing verification before publication. Two
held. One did not.

- **Psalm 58 omitted from lectionaries** — confirmed. Absent from the three-year Revised
  Common Lectionary, and also removed from the United Methodist Hymnal, the Episcopal Sunday
  Lectionary, and the Roman Catholic Liturgy of the Hours.
- **Bracton at Fox p. 27** — confirmed against the source PDF.
- **"Jesus uses the word rarely"** — **failed.** *Ponēros* is common in the Gospels (the evil
  generation, the evil one, the evil eye). Corrected to the narrower and defensible claim: he
  applies it to individuals less often than expected, and the parable usage is narrow.

The same search that verified the lectionary claim surfaced Bonhoeffer, which produced the
article's strongest paragraph. **Fact-checking is not only defensive.**

## What a collaborator should take from this

1. **The theology is worked out in conversation before any prose is drafted.** Structure is
   proposed in beats, approved, then drafted one beat at a time with approval at each stage.
2. **Theology arrives late.** No thesis in the opening paragraph. The reflection does not
   explain what it found until the reader has felt the problem.
3. **Nothing is invented.** Not a person, not a detail, not a moment. If the convention
   requires something the material does not contain, the convention yields.
4. **The author's discomfort is diagnostic.** Three times in this production the author
   identified a problem by feel — *it is more you than me*, *not the right ending*, *take it
   out* — and was right each time before the reason had been articulated. The collaboration's
   job is to work out what is underneath the objection, not to defend the draft.
5. **Cut rather than add.** Every length decision in this set went toward the shorter version.
6. **Verify before publishing.** Three claims were flagged; one was wrong. Had it gone up, a
   reader with a concordance would have found it in a minute.
