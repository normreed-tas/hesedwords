## B. Groeschel

**9. The book.** **CONFIRMED.** Craig Groeschel, *It: How Churches and Leaders Can Get It and Keep It* (Grand Rapids: Zondervan, 2008). Google Books gives Zondervan, published 2 September 2008, hardcover 190 pages. The place of publication is Zondervan's usual one and was not read off the book.

**10. "I don't know. Really, I don't."** **CONFIRMED** in the book. The passage reads: "By now you're probably asking, *What is it?* My answer is … / I don't know. / Really, I don't." The two sentences are on separate lines in the book, so quoting them run together is fair. **No page number:** the Google Books preview does not show page numbers, and the hit would not open to a numbered page. Cite the preface without a page, or Norm can read the number off his screenshot.

**11. The paraphrases.** Both hold as reported speech.
- The book: "Most churches want *it*. Few have *it*." The draft's "Most churches want it, but few have it" is close.
- The book: "Without a doubt, *it* is a gift from God, who is its only source." The draft's "it comes from God, and from nowhere else" is a fair paraphrase.

**12. The list. EDITORIAL, for Norm.** The book does have a list, and it is not the draft's. Under the heading "Easy to Misinterpret" Groeschel says it is "fairly obvious what *it* is not", and names what a visitor to a growing church sees: "videos, buildings, fancy kids' rooms, and so on". Further on: "*It* is not being on television, being on the Internet, or being on book and magazine covers", and "*It* doesn't follow a particular model." He ends: "Like it or not, *it* is not the result of external factors."

Set against the draft's "good music, a strong leader, a big building, a clever programme":
- **A building** is in the book.
- **Music, a strong leader and a clever programme** were not found in the passages the preview shows. "Doesn't follow a particular model" is the nearest thing to "a clever programme".
- The phrase **"But that's not it"** was not found in the book. It may belong to the sermon only.

The preview shows only parts of these pages, so the book's list may be longer than this. Options for Norm: use the book's items (videos, buildings, fancy kids' rooms); or say "He went through a list of things it might be" with no items; or keep the sermon as memory and name only the building. The sermon and the book need not have had the same list.

## C. Greek, Latin and Septuagint

| # | Claim | Result |
|---|---|---|
| 14 | Both second-person forms in 4:1 are plural | **CONFIRMED.** ὑμᾶς is accusative plural; ἐκλήθητε is second person plural, aorist passive. |
| 15 | The cognate pairs | **CONFIRMED.** 4:1 τῆς κλήσεως ἧς ἐκλήθητε; 4:4 ἐκλήθητε ἐν μιᾷ ἐλπίδι τῆς κλήσεως ὑμῶν. Noun κλῆσις and verb καλέω in each. |
| 16 | ἀξίως in Eph 4:1 and Phil 1:27 | **CONFIRMED.** The same adverb in both: ἀξίως περιπατῆσαι (Eph 4:1); ἀξίως τοῦ εὐαγγελίου τοῦ Χριστοῦ πολιτεύεσθε (Phil 1:27). Note that WEB translates it "worthily" in Ephesians and "worthy" in Philippians, so the reader will not see the match in English. |
| 17 | ματαιότης | **CONFIRMED.** Eph 4:17 ἐν ματαιότητι τοῦ νοὸς αὐτῶν. LXX Eccl 1:2: Ματαιότης ματαιοτήτων, εἶπεν ὁ ἐκκλησιαστής, ματαιότης ματαιοτήτων, τὰ πάντα ματαιότης. The Hebrew there is הֶבֶל (five times in the verse). The claim of a shared word holds; nothing here shows an allusion, and the prose does not claim one. |
| 18 | *vocation* from Latin *vocatio* | **CONFIRMED** (Wiktionary: Middle English *vocacioun*, from Old French *vocation*, from Latin *vocātiō*). That *vocātiō* comes from *vocāre*, "to call", is standard but was not opened. |
