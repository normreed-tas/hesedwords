## The opening observation

Norm's own, from the reading: the parables in Mark 4 are all about seed growing, and are
grouped deliberately rather than delivered in that sequence. If Peter stands behind the
account, the grouping is his or Mark's, not the occasion's.

The three seed pieces: the sower (4:3–20), the seed growing while the man sleeps
(4:26–29), the mustard seed (4:30–32). The lamp-and-measure block (4:21–25) sits between
them and is not agricultural — but says the same thing (*nothing hidden except to be made
manifest*), which is what a seed does.

Norm's second observation, and the one the session ran on: the hiddenness of the parables
and the hiddenness of the seed are the same shape. Understanding arrives, but not by being
watched for.

## Where the session went

1. **The grouping is editorial.** 4:13 (*how then will you understand all the
   parables?*) makes the sower a key rather than an item. 4:33 (*with many such parables*)
   concedes selection. Papias's "not in order" cuts in Norm's direction.
2. **4:11–12 is the hard place.** Mark has *hina* (purpose) where Matthew has *hoti*
   (cause), plus *mēpote* — lest they turn and be forgiven. Mark chose or kept the harder
   construction.
3. **The private explanation doesn't function as a key.** 8:17 aims the same Isaiah
   language at the twelve. So the inside/outside line isn't between informed and ignorant.
   Being told plainly does not exempt you; it still takes a life.
4. **Two hiddennesses, tangled.** The *messianic* concealment has a stated terminus —
   9:9, *until the Son of Man has risen* — and ends at the trial, where *egō eimi* (14:62)
   is the first plain public claim, made at the one moment it cannot be misconstrued as a
   bid for power. The *seed's* hiddenness did not end. Paul still couldn't say how.
5. **Why the apostles changed mode.** They preach announcement, not parable — right form
   for news, and obedience to 9:9. But they inherited the sower's *shape*: *I planted,
   Apollos watered, God gave the growth* is 4:26–29 in apostolic idiom. They took the job
   rather than the method.
6. **Norm's correction, and it landed.** If the parables were merely provisional they
   should read as period pieces. They don't. So the honest position is that one hiddenness
   expired and one didn't — and Mark doesn't explain the apostles' change of mode. My
   earlier account was filling a silence.
7. **Church as mustard seed.** Norm: hold both parables together rather than separating
   them. China after 1951, South America, the early church, and the generational
   death-and-return. Same event at different distances — unwatched growth close up,
   visible tree from a hundred years away. The corollary: you can only ever say *it grew*
   looking backwards. In the field, at the time, you are asleep and it looks like nothing.

## The Greek

- **4:11 *dedotai*** — perfect passive. Not achieved; handed over, with standing result.
  The disciples' position is receptive, not earned. *Established.*
- **4:11 *mystērion*** — singular, and the only occurrence in Mark. One thing, not a set of
  secrets. Daniel 2 background (*raz*) carries disclosure at a chosen time inside the word.
  *Established* for the singular and the hapax; *reading-within-frame* for the Daniel
  weighting.
- **4:12 *epistrephō*** — the physical word: turn round, face the other way. Not the usual
  repentance term. *Established.*
- **4:33 *kathōs ēdynanto akouein*** — according to their capacity to hear. Eleven verses
  after *lest they turn*, and pointing the other way. Not a wall; a dosage. *Established*
  for the lexis; *reading-within-frame* for reading it as accommodation against 4:12.
- **4:34 *epelyen*** — imperfect of *epilyō*, to loosen or untie. Habitual: he kept untying
  them. The word Mark chose for private teaching is not a word for handing over a key.
  *Established* for the lexis. The strongest single find of the session.
- **4:12 and the Targum on Isaiah 6:10** — the Targum's ending (*and it be forgiven them*)
  may lie behind Mark's *mēpote* rather than the Hebrew or LXX. `[verify]` — working from
  memory on the wording; must not be asserted without checking.

What I could not recall anyone arguing: the specific escalation Norm was circling —
sower → autonomous growth → mustard seed as a progression in which each parable removes
something further from the hearer's control. May be in Marcus. May be Norm's.

## The two candidates

**1. Why don't we teach this way?** The piece with the most in it, and the least written
elsewhere. Jesus used stories that concealed; we use illustrations that reveal. He made it
harder; we make it easier. An illustration serves a point that exists before it and
survives its removal — a parable has no prior point, which is why he could answer *what
does it mean* with more parable. So we are not using a weaker version of his method but a
different one.

The turn: our preference may not be pedagogical. A parable costs the teacher control. You
cannot check that it landed, and you may be misunderstood and have to let that stand. We
may prefer illustration because we want to know we have been effective. That is 4:27 aimed
at the preacher — *he does not know how* as a verse about teaching.

Register: practitioners, not the parables shelf. Article, probably. Note that Norm's
audience is nearer the crowd's position than the apostles' — people who have heard the
news, agree with it, and are unmoved — which argues *for* the parable rather than against.

**2. You can only say *it grew* looking backwards.** True and useful, but not new to
anyone who has read 4:26–29 well. Reads as the second half of candidate 1 rather than a
piece of its own. Holds the China / South America / generational-return material, and
John 12:24 — the seed goes under the ground and stops looking like anything before it does
anything, said in the same week as the trial. Same word to a shrinking parish as to a
swelling one.
