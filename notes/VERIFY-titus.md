## A. Scripture

**1. 2 Cor 2:12–13.**
- ESV: "When I came to Troas to preach the gospel of Christ, even though a door was opened for
  me in the Lord, my spirit was not at rest because I did not find my brother Titus there. So
  I took leave of them and went on to Macedonia."
- KJV: "…a door was opened unto me of the Lord, I had no rest in my spirit, because I found not
  Titus my brother: but taking my leave of them, I went from thence into Macedonia."

**"He did not go through it."** The text says the door was open and that he left. It does not
say he preached nothing at all in Troas. As a statement that he did not stay and use the
opening, it's fair. As "he never preached there", it says more than the text.

**2. 2 Cor 7:5–6.**
- KJV: "our flesh had no rest, but we were troubled on every side; without were fightings,
  within were fears. Nevertheless God, that comforteth those that are cast down, comforted us
  by the coming of Titus".
- ESV: "our bodies had no rest… fighting without and fear within. But God, who comforts the
  downcast, comforted us by the coming of Titus".

**3. Gal 2:1–5.**
- 2:1: "with Barnabas, taking Titus along with me".
- 2:3: "not forced to be circumcised, though he was a Greek".
- 2:4: "false brothers secretly brought in… to spy out our freedom".
- 2:5, ESV: "to them we did not yield in submission even for a moment, so that the truth of
  the gospel might be preserved for you." KJV: "that the truth of the gospel might continue
  with you."

**The draft's rendering is fair.** It reads: "If Titus were circumcised, it would have settled
that a Greek had to become a Jew before he could belong to Christ."
- **Paul's stated reason** is "the truth of the gospel".
- **That this truth *is* "a Gentile need not become a Jew"** is made explicit a few verses on,
  where Paul uses the same phrase: Cephas was "not in step with the truth of the gospel", and
  he asks "how can you force the Gentiles to live like Jews?" (2:14, ESV; not on the per-verse
  pages I opened, quoted from the chapter).

So it's a rendering of the content, supported by the chapter, not an overreach. Keep the
sentence presenting it as what was at stake, which it does, rather than as Paul's words.

**4. 2 Cor 2:3–4.**
- ESV: "For I wrote to you out of much affliction and anguish of heart and with many tears".
- KJV: "For out of much affliction and anguish of heart I wrote unto you with many tears".

**Caveat.** Calling this "the severe letter" (a lost letter between 1 and 2 Corinthians) is the
majority reading, but some identify it with 1 Corinthians. If the prose names it "the severe
letter", that's defensible. If it builds anything on the letter being lost, it rests on an
inference.

**5–11.** Wording confirmed as the brief states:
- 8:6, ESV: "as he had started, so he should complete among you this act of grace". KJV: "as
  he had begun, so he would also finish".
- 12:18, KJV: "walked we not in the same spirit? walked we not in the same steps?" ESV: "Did we
  not act in the same spirit? Did we not take the same steps?"
- 7:13–15: Titus's spirit "refreshed by you all"; "the obedience of you all, how you received
  him with fear and trembling"; "his affection for you is even greater".
- 1 Cor 4:17: Timothy, "my beloved and faithful child in the Lord, to remind you of my ways".
  KJV: "beloved son, and faithful in the Lord".
- 1 Cor 16:10–11: KJV "that he may be with you without fear… Let no man therefore despise
  him". ESV "put him at ease among you… let no one despise him". Both are of Timothy.
- 2 Tim 4:10: "Titus to Dalmatia". KJV: "Titus unto Dalmatia".
- Titus 1:5, KJV: "set in order the things that are wanting, and ordain elders in every city".
  ESV: "put what remained into order, and appoint elders in every town".

**Translation.** The page's translation isn't set. The brief's own phrasings mostly follow the
**KJV** ("no rest in his spirit", "fightings without, fears within", "comforts the cast down",
"walked we not in the same spirit", "without fear", "the things wanting"). Whichever version
the conventions settle on, quotations should match it.

## B. Inferences

**12. The earlier visit — CORRECTED.**
- **What the text supports.** 8:6 says Titus had *begun* (*proenērxato*) the collection among
  them, so he had worked in Corinth before. Read with 8:10 and 9:2 ("since last year"), most
  commentators take it that Titus started the collection on an earlier visit. That much is
  mainstream.
- **What it does not support.** "Before the trouble" and "on ordinary business" go beyond the
  text. Commentators divide on whether Titus started the collection a year earlier, or during
  the visit on which he carried the severe letter, which was during the trouble.
- **Suggested wording:** "Titus had been to Corinth before. He had started the collection
  there (8:6)." That says only what the text gives. This is from the text and general
  knowledge of the commentary debate; I opened no commentary for it.

**13. Galatians 2 first in the sequence — CONFIRMED.**
- The Jerusalem visit of Gal 2:1 is identified either with the famine visit (Acts 11:30) or
  with the council (Acts 15).
- On either view it falls about AD 48–49. The Corinthian crisis of 2 Corinthians comes later,
  in the mid-50s. That holds whether Galatians was written early (about 48) or late (mid-50s).
- So "first in sequence of events" is defensible. Dates are from general knowledge, not
  opened. The prose gives none, so nothing needs a citation.

**14. "Paul does not write it about Titus" — CONFIRMED.**
- Paul commends Titus warmly: 2 Cor 8:16–17, and 8:23, "my partner and fellow worker for your
  benefit".
- But nothing said of Titus resembles the protective language for Timothy: "without fear",
  "let no one despise him". All 13 New Testament mentions of Titus were checked by lemma.

## C. Counts

**15.** Lemma Τίτος, SBLGNT:

| Book | Count | Verses |
|---|---|---|
| **2 Corinthians** | **9** | 2:13; 7:6; 7:13; 7:14; 8:6; 8:16; 8:23; 12:18 (twice) |
| Galatians | 2 | 2:1, 2:3 |
| 2 Timothy | 1 | 4:10 |
| Titus | 1 | 1:4 |
| **New Testament total** | **13** | |

So "again and again" in 2 Corinthians is fair: nine of the thirteen. If a number helps the
sentence, it's **nine times**.

**16. Absent from Acts — CONFIRMED.**
- Titus is not named anywhere in Acts.
- **Titius Justus (Acts 18:7)** is a different name, *Titios* (Τίτιος).
- **The trap.** The SBLGNT marks a textual variant at that word. Some manuscripts read "Titus
  Justus", and others have "Justus" alone (as the KJV does).
- That variant is why some have linked him to Titus. The usual modern view is that he is a
  different man; one suggestion links him with Gaius of Corinth (Rom 16:23; 1 Cor 1:14).
- **Recommendation: leave him out.** A footnote would have to explain a manuscript variant to
  say that he probably isn't Titus. If he is mentioned at all, "Titus is not named in Acts"
  must stay true, and it does.

## D. Tradition

**17. First bishop of Crete — CONFIRMED, with its date.** Eusebius, *Church History* 3.4.6
(written in the early 4th century):

> "Timothy, so it is recorded, was the first to receive the episcopate of the parish in
> Ephesus, Titus of the churches in Crete."

Two points:
- Eusebius says "**so it is recorded**". He is reporting an earlier record, not his own
  knowledge.
- He says "the churches in Crete", not a single see. "First bishop of Crete" is a fair
  shorthand.

Citation: Eusebius, *Ecclesiastical History* 3.4.6.

**18. Died on Crete at a great age — REMOVE.** I could not open a primary source. The
tradition is usually traced to a late *Acts of Titus*, but that is from memory, not opened.
Under the brief's rule, it comes out.

**19. Honoured at Gortyna — REMOVE as written.** No source opened. (From general knowledge
only, not opened: there is a ruined basilica of St Titus at Gortyn, usually dated to the 6th
century. If Norm wants that in, it needs a source opened first.)

**20. Jerome on Titus and Luke — NOT FOUND. Remove the sentence.**
- Jerome's chapter on Luke (*De Viris Illustribus* 7) calls Luke "a physician of Antioch" and
  Paul's companion.
- It applies to Luke the line "We send with him a brother whose praise in the gospel is among
  all the churches" (2 Cor 8:18).
- **It says nothing of Titus**, and nothing of Luke and Titus being related. Titus appears
  elsewhere in the work only as the addressee of a letter (ch. 5); ch. 2's "Titus" is the
  emperor.

The drafting session's suspicion of a conflation looks right. The likely source is this: in 2
Cor 8:18 "the brother" sent *with Titus* is identified, by Jerome and others, as Luke. Some
much later readers took "the brother" to mean Titus's own brother. That is a modern proposal
(often credited to Alexander Souter, but I did not open it), not Jerome's. As the brief says,
remove the sentence rather than reattribute it. Reattributing it would be Norm's call.

**21. "None of it is early, and none of it is certain."**
- **"None of it is certain"** holds.
- **"None of it is early"** depends on what "early" means. The bishopric is in Eusebius,
  written about 260 years after Titus, and Eusebius cites an older record.
- If items 18–20 come out, the paragraph rests on Eusebius alone, and the sentence should fit
  him. **Suggested wording:** "The earliest of it is three centuries late, and none of it is
  certain." Or keep "none of it is early" with Eusebius named and dated, so the reader can
  judge.
