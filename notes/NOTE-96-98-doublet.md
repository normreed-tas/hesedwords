# NOTE — The 96 / 98 doublet

**Held.** No Selah, and probably not a reflection at all — closer in kind to
`NOTE-chapter-divisions.md`: an editorial question about how the collection was
assembled, with a theological tail.

Arose 10 September 2026 from the Psalm 98 morning.

## The observation

Psalm 98 and Psalm 96 are close to the same psalm.

Shared elements:

- **Sing to the LORD a new song** — 96:1, 98:1
- **Declared among the nations / in the sight of the nations** — 96:3, 98:2
- **Salvation** as the thing made known — 96:2, 98:2–3
- **Creation summoned** — sea, world, field, trees (96:11–12); sea, rivers, hills (98:7–8)
- **He comes to judge the earth** — 96:13, 98:9, in nearly identical wording
- **With righteousness / with equity** — 96:13, 98:9

A version of 96 also appears in **1 Chronicles 16:23–33**, in David's psalm at the
bringing up of the ark — itself stitched together from 105, 96 and 106.

*Warrant: Established* — the parallels are on the page and the Chronicles parallel is
explicit.

## The question

Somebody assembling the Psalter kept both. That is the interesting part. A collection
edited for economy would have dropped one, or merged them. Instead 96 and 98 sit two
psalms apart with 97 between them, saying much the same thing in much the same words.

Options:

1. **Liturgical.** Both were in use, in different settings or by different communities,
   and the collection preserves usage rather than tidying it. Plausible and dull.
2. **Deliberate placement.** 96–99 may be a set — the enthronement group — and the
   repetition is structural rather than accidental. Would need the shape of 93–100
   worked out properly.
3. **The repetition is the point.** Some things are said twice because once is not
   enough, and the Psalter is not a reference work trying to avoid redundancy.

*Warrant: Suggestive–thin* on all three. I have not done the work.

## Why it matters beyond curiosity

Two tails worth following:

- **It bears on the site's own practice.** Norm is content with multiple entries on the
  same psalm and with psalms that get nothing. If the Psalter itself keeps near-duplicates
  rather than consolidating, that is not a defence of the practice so much as a precedent
  for it. Worth noting; not worth leaning on hard.
- **It bears on the 3,000-repetitions finding** already developed for the Psalm 81
  article (structural argument about psalm-selection, never drafted). Same territory:
  what the collection chose to keep and repeat. These two notes may want to be one
  article rather than two.

## Loose ends

- Work the shape of **Psalms 93–100** as a group before drafting anything. Are 96 and 98
  in the same sub-collection, and does 97 sit between them for a reason?
- **Other Psalter doublets** to gather: 14 // 53 (nearly verbatim), 40:13–17 // 70,
  57:7–11 + 60:5–12 // 108, 31:1–3 // 71:1–3. `[verify]` the list and the extent of
  overlap in each. A piece on doublets generally is stronger than a piece on this pair.
- The **1 Chronicles 16 composite** is a third data point of a different kind — not a
  doublet but a psalm quarried for a new occasion. Whether that belongs in the same
  argument is undecided.
- Nothing here is safe to publish until the doublet list is checked. The whole argument
  rests on how common the phenomenon is, and I do not know.

## Relation to existing notes

Sits alongside `NOTE-chapter-divisions.md` (reading obstacles created by later editorial
decisions) and the undrafted Psalm 81 material (psalm-selection and repetition). All
three are about the shape the collection was given rather than the content of any one
piece. If they converge, that is an article on how the Psalter was put together and what
the assembly is doing — which would be a different register from anything on the site so
far, and would need the audience question settled first.
