# The half Luke drops — Malachi 4:6, Luke 1:17, Sirach 48:10 — held note

**Status: held.** Opened 21 September 2026 out of the verification work for the article
*Knowing Where He Came From*, which cites Malachi 4:5–6 in passing. The article says
Luke "picks it up", which is accurate and stays as written. This note is the thing the
checking turned up that the article had no room for.

---

## The observation

Malachi ends the Old Testament with a turning that goes **both ways**. Luke quotes it and
keeps only one direction. And he is not the first to do that.

| | |
|---|---|
| **Malachi 4:6** | he will turn back the heart of fathers to sons **and the heart of sons to their fathers**, lest I come and strike the land with total destruction |
| **Sirach 48:10** | ἐπιστρέψαι καρδίαν πατρὸς πρὸς υἱὸν **καὶ καταστῆσαι φυλὰς Ιακωβ** — to turn the heart of the father to the son, **and to restore the tribes of Jacob** |
| **Luke 1:17** | ἐπιστρέψαι καρδίας πατέρων ἐπὶ τέκνα **καὶ ἀπειθεῖς ἐν φρονήσει δικαίων** — to turn the hearts of fathers to children, **and the disobedient to the wisdom of the just** |

All verified 21 Sep 2026 — Hebrew from STEPBible TAHOT, Greek from Rahlfs and the
morphgnt SBLGNT. See the dated section at the foot.

Three things follow.

**The first clause is stable and the second is not.** Fathers-to-children survives every
retelling, nearly word for word. Children-to-fathers survives none of them.

**Sirach is pre-Christian**, second century BC, so this is not a Christian adjustment of
a Jewish text. There was already a habit of citing Malachi's Elijah oracle with the
second half replaced. Luke stands inside that habit rather than inventing something.

**Luke and Sirach share the verb.** Both use ἐπιστρέψαι. Whatever Luke is following, it
is closer to Ben Sira's form of the oracle than to a fresh translation of the Hebrew.

## Why it is worth a piece

The dropped half is the one about **children turning back to fathers** — which is to say,
the one about knowing where you came from. That is the half that does not get carried
forward, in either retelling, and it is exactly the half the prison work keeps running
into. *Knowing Where He Came From* argues that a man who cannot say where he came from
cannot kneel in front of anybody. Malachi promised that turning. The tradition that
quotes him lets it go.

That is either a large thing or a coincidence of transmission, and the note should not
decide which before the work is done.

## What this note is NOT claiming

- **Not** that Luke is suppressing anything. The simplest account is that he is citing a
  known form of the oracle, and Sirach shows that form existed.
- **Not** that Malachi's second clause was lost. It is there in the Hebrew, and Jewish
  liturgical practice repeats 4:5 after 4:6 precisely so the book does not end on the
  curse — so the verse was being handled carefully, not neglected.
- **Not** that ἀπειθεῖς ἐν φρονήσει δικαίων is a weaker idea. It may be a deliberate
  widening: from a family reconciliation to a whole generation's disobedience.

## Loose ends

- What the LXX of Malachi 4:6 itself reads, and whether it already differs from the
  Hebrew. **Not yet checked** — this note has the Hebrew and the two citations, not the
  Greek Malachi. Do that before drafting anything.
- Whether any other Second Temple text cites the oracle, and which half it keeps.
- Whether Luke 1:16 (turning many of the children of Israel to the Lord their God)
  is doing the dropped half's work in a different register, one verse earlier.
- Whether Malachi's "lest I come and strike the land" belongs in the piece at all. It is
  the clause that makes the turning urgent, and both retellings drop it too.
- Kind not settled: article, or a reflection that stays with the one observation.

## Verification — 21 September 2026 (Claude Code)

**Malachi 4:6** (Hebrew 3:24), from TAHOT: *and he will turn back [the] heart of fathers
to sons and [the] heart of sons to fathers/their, lest I should come and I will strike
[obj.] the/land total destruction.* Both directions present.

**Luke 1:17**, SBLGNT: καὶ αὐτὸς προελεύσεται ἐνώπιον αὐτοῦ ἐν πνεύματι καὶ δυνάμει
Ἠλίου, ἐπιστρέψαι καρδίας πατέρων ἐπὶ τέκνα καὶ ἀπειθεῖς ἐν φρονήσει δικαίων, ἑτοιμάσαι
κυρίῳ λαὸν κατεσκευασμένον. "In the spirit and power of Elijah" is verbatim.

**Sirach 48:10**, Rahlfs: ὁ καταγραφεὶς ἐν ἐλεγμοῖς εἰς καιροὺς κοπάσαι ὀργὴν πρὸ θυμοῦ
ἐπιστρέψαι καρδίαν πατρὸς πρὸς υἱὸν καὶ καταστῆσαι φυλὰς Ιακωβ.
