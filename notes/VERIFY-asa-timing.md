# Asa: the timing check (2 Chronicles 14–16)

This is the pre-drafting check for the claim that the embassy was slower than the prayer would have been. Every point below names the source that was opened. Where no source was opened, it says so.

## 1. Ramah to Jerusalem
**Confirmed.**

Ramah is identified with er-Ram. The sources put it about 5 to 6 miles north of Jerusalem:
- Bible Hub atlas: 5 miles.
- Encyclopedia of the Bible (Bible Gateway): about 5½ miles.
- Jewish Encyclopedia: 9 km.
- Eusebius: 6 Roman miles.

Its control of traffic comes from two places:
- The text itself: "that he might permit no one to go out or come in to Asa" (16:1).
- Jamieson-Fausset-Brown (Bible Hub): "the high road to and from Jerusalem passed by Ramah", "about six miles north".

The Hebrew of 16:1 reads לְבִלְתִּי תֵּת יוֹצֵא וָבָא ("so as not to allow going out or coming in").

## 2. Jerusalem to Damascus
**Weakly sourced only.**

- A Bible Hub-type reference, surfaced through search, gives about 135 miles (220 km) as the crow flies and a 5 to 7 day journey.
- A Christianity Today piece gives 18 miles a day as the average for walkers on the Persian Royal Road. That is a later period and a different road.

No source was opened that gives figures for a royal embassy carrying treasure, and none for a reply.

Arithmetic on those figures, not sourced:
- about a week each way;
- about two weeks for the round trip, before any time spent in negotiation at Damascus.

Route note from the Jewish Encyclopedia: the northern road ran from Jerusalem through Beth-el and Shechem, then via Beth-shean to Damascus. Beth-el lies past Ramah, which suggests the embassy could not simply use the road Baasha was blocking. No source discusses which route the embassy took.

## 3. Ben-hadad's march (16:4)
**Not checked.**

No source on the distances from Damascus to Ijon, Dan or Abel-maim was opened, and none on how long Ben-hadad would need to muster and march.

## 4. Time markers in the Hebrew of 16:1-6
**None.**

The Hebrew was read from Mechon Mamre. The only time marker is the year in 16:1: בִּשְׁנַת שְׁלֹשִׁים וָשֵׁשׁ ("in the thirty-sixth year").

Every step after that is joined by wayyiqtol verbs, "and he brought out… and sent… and Ben-hadad listened… and sent… and they struck". Verse 5 adds וַיְהִי כִּשְׁמֹעַ בַּעְשָׁא ("when Baasha heard"), which sets out the order of events but not how long they took.

The sequence is compressed, and the text gives no elapsed time.

## 5. Commentators on how long the embassy took
**None found.**

The Bible Hub commentary page for 16:2 was opened. It carries Ellicott, Matthew Henry, Barnes, JFB, Poole, Gill, Geneva, Cambridge, Pulpit and Keil & Delitzsch. None of them remarks on how long the embassy took. Their discussion of time is about the "36th year" problem:
- Keil & Delitzsch read it as a copyist's error for the 16th year.
- JFB reckons it from the division of the kingdom.
- Gill notes that Baasha was already dead by the 36th year.

That silence shows only that none of them addresses the question. It does not show that any of them agrees with the claim.

## 6. The order of events in 14:9-11
**Confirmed.** ESV, from Bible Hub:
- v9: "Zerah the Ethiopian came out against them… and came as far as Mareshah."
- v10: "And Asa went out to meet him, and they drew up their lines of battle in the Valley of Zephathah at Mareshah."
- v11: "And Asa cried to the LORD his God…"

The prayer comes after both armies are drawn up and the enemy is present. The answer comes at once in v12: "So the LORD defeated the Ethiopians."

## What this supports
**The reduced form is supported: "the prayer was immediate; the embassy was not."**
- The prayer and the victory sit next to each other in the text.
- The embassy needed at least a round trip to Damascus and a campaign in the far north, which was at least weeks of work. The text gives no time at all.

**The full form is not supported: "asking would have been quicker."**
- It compares the embassy with a prayer that was never made.
- No source gives a duration for the embassy.
- No commentator makes the comparison.

The choice is Norm's.
