# NOTE-batemans-bay.md

**Held.** Added 11 September 2026. No piece attached. Recorded so the story does not
have to be reconstructed from memory a second time.

Raised in a session on Mark 9–10 (*What Do You Want Me to Do for You* — the doubled
question at 10:36 and 10:51). Offered as a candidate Selah for that piece and declined;
see **Why it was declined** below. The material is sound. The fit was wrong.

## The account

As given by Norm, 11 September 2026, lightly tidied for spelling only. Sequence and
emphasis are his.

Not long after he began following Jesus, Norm was struggling with whether he should
rekindle a relationship with the woman who would become his wife. He was, by his own
description, somewhat fanatical about his faith at the time, and genuinely seeking
whether he should entertain the thought of marriage at all or — as he then understood
the Bible to be saying — remain celibate.

He had met Cathy in the navy, where she was the nursing sister at the naval college and
he was the captain's secretary. She had been overseas for a couple of years studying
midwifery. They had parted as good friends before she left, with no deep commitment and
no plans to continue the relationship, though they stayed in touch.

Because there was no commitment, Cathy returned to Australia and made plans to move to
Perth for community nursing in WA. Before the move she planned to visit friends. Norm
invited her to Canberra and arranged for her to stay with close friends in his new
church.

Complicating it: he had been selected to try out for the role of ADC to the
Governor-General. One of three navy candidates who would spend two days and a night
living at Government House, meeting the Governor-General and his wife. The interview
stipulated that candidates were not permitted an active relationship during the twelve
months in the role.

So Cathy's visit arrived at a pivotal moment, and as a new Christian he was struggling
to know what God wanted.

He set aside the weekend before meeting her to spend a couple of days in a caravan park
at Batemans Bay, a few hours' drive from where they would meet, to pray and fast about
what he should do.

He did not get absolute clarity. He came away with a sense that he should remain
unattached, and he intentionally did not try to develop the relationship.

He was not selected for the ADC role. He kept the condition anyway. Cathy, during the
visit, made her own decision to follow Jesus and stayed in Canberra rather than going to
Perth. Almost twelve months to the day, Norm asked her to marry him.

Norm's own summary: *"The decision I made was important — but not permanent. However it
did define what following Jesus looked like."*

## Why it was declined as the Selah for *What Do You Want Me to Do for You*

Recorded so the same fit isn't attempted again.

- That piece turns on the doubled question in Mark 10 — the sons of Zebedee asking for
  the thrones, Bartimaeus asking to see. The Selah it needs is the sound of one's own
  voice asking for the wrong thing. There is no wrong thing in this account. The one
  candidate for a status ask, the ADC role, Norm did not get and did not grieve.
- The account resolves. It ends in a marriage that vindicates the decision. The Mark
  passage ends with a blind man following a man on his way to Jerusalem to die, and the
  disciples still not seeing it. A resolving Selah would undercut the text.

## The two things worth keeping

Both are live. Neither has a piece.

**1. Keeping a rule after the thing holding it up has gone.**
The ADC role fell through and the condition stayed. Norm imposed it on himself. The open
question — not yet answered, and the reason this isn't drafted — is *why*. Obedience?
Stubbornness? Or because he had said it to God and the saying was itself the thing? These
are three different pieces. Until he can say which it was, there is nothing to write.

**2. Going for clarity and getting a sense instead.**
Two days of prayer and fasting at Batemans Bay produced no absolute answer. He came away
with a leaning and acted on it. This is the honest report of most of thirty years and the
part most testimony leaves out. Possibly the stronger of the two, and possibly a piece in
its own right on guidance — what it is actually like to be led, as against how it is
usually described.

Note the structural rhyme with Mark, which may be why the story surfaced in that session
even though it doesn't fit that piece: the disciples walk the road for two chapters
without seeing where it goes, and are not rebuked for it. Following precedes seeing. That
is the same shape as Batemans Bay. If a piece comes out of this material, that is the
likelier door — not the marriage, and not the decision.
