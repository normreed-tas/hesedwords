## Study A — who holds the glass, canon-wide

All 64 occurrences were read in context. Where an appraiser is identifiable:

| God | Human |
|---|---|
| **Isa 43:4**, "precious in my eyes" | 1 Sam 18:30, David's name esteemed (people, implied) |
| **Ps 116:15**, "in the sight of the LORD" | 1 Sam 26:21, "my life was precious in your eyes" (David, of Saul's life) |
| | 2 Kgs 1:13, 1:14, the captain's plea to Elijah, ×2 |
| | Ps 72:14, the king |
| | Ps 36:7, the psalmist |
| | Ps 139:17, the psalmist |
| | Esth 1:20, wives honouring husbands |
| | **Esth 6:3, 6 (×2), 7, 9 (×2), 11**: "the man whom the king delights to honour", ×7 |
| | **Zech 11:13 (×2)**: "the lordly price at which I was priced by them", thirty pieces of silver |
| **2** | **about 17** (10 without Esther) |

**Edge cases, not counted:**
- **Isa 13:12**, "I will make people more rare than fine gold". God *causes* scarcity; he is
  not appraising.
- **Jer 15:19**, "if you utter what is precious". Jeremiah is sorting words, not valuing
  something.
- **Job 28:10**, "his eye sees every precious thing". A miner seeing, not valuing.

**No appraiser, about 42.** Costly stones and jewels, the Temple and palace stones, wealth,
rarity (1 Sam 3:1), splendour, honour as a social fact, and the Psalter's 49:8, 49:12, 49:20,
37:20 and 45:9.

**Verdict: the claim HOLDS.** "Ask who holds the glass, and the answer is nearly always a
person" is true canon-wide, and the two exceptions are exactly the two the study turns to.

**Optional, not in the brief: Zech 11:13** is the sharpest human appraisal in the canon.
People put a price on *God*, the shepherd, and it is thirty pieces of silver, "the lordly
price". Matthew takes it up (27:9–10). It is Norm's call whether it earns a place. It would
sit naturally just before "And then God takes the glass up himself".

## Reflection A4 — "mostly" FAILS

| Kind | Verses | Count |
|---|---|---|
| Things that do not last | 49:12 and 49:20 (pomp that will not remain); 37:20 (splendour that vanishes) | **3** |
| Unaffordable rather than passing | 49:8, the ransom | 1 |
| Of God's things | 36:7, 139:17 | 2 |
| God appraising | 116:15 | 1 |
| The king appraising | 72:14 | 1 |
| Honour, not passing | 45:9 | 1 |

That is 3 (or 4) of 9, not "mostly".

**Rewrite, not soften.** For example: "In the Psalms it is used of a ransom nobody can pay, of
a man in his pomp, of the splendour of the pastures, which goes up like smoke." That drops
the count claim and keeps the list.

## Reflection A5 — Tolkien

**Footnote citation.**
- J. R. R. Tolkien, *The Hobbit* (1937), ch. 5, "Riddles in the Dark".
- *The Lord of the Rings*, *The Fellowship of the Ring*, book I, ch. 2, "The Shadow of the
  Past".

No edition is given, as the brief asked. A two-word phrase needs no more than a reference to
the work.

**The wording.**
- **Tolkien's Gollum calls the Ring "my precious"** (Wikipedia: "Gollum calls the Ring 'my
  precious'").
- **In *The Hobbit* he also calls *himself* "my precious".**
- The hissed "precioussss" spelling is not confirmed as Tolkien's in anything I opened. The
  draft's plain *my precious* is safe.

**"Took him apart, slowly, over a long time" is fair.** He held the Ring for centuries.
Wikipedia gives 556 years from finding to destruction, and says it twisted his body and mind
and stretched his life far past its span. Gandalf's account is in "The Shadow of the Past",
where he "loved and hated [the Ring], as he loved and hated himself".

**Flag: "The ring let itself be held. That was the lie in it."** As I remember it, *The
Shadow of the Past* has Gandalf say the opposite: the Ring *left* Gollum. It chose to slip
away, as it slipped from Isildur. **I did not open Tolkien's text to confirm this.** The
article I read did not cover it.
- **If the memory is right,** the sentence still works as a reading: it let itself be held
  for a while, then dropped him.
- **But a Tolkien reader may stop at it.** Worth checking against the book before the prose
  locks, or wording it so it does not claim the Ring stays held.

## F1 — duplication between the two pieces

The brief allows Sheba and Proverbs in both. Beyond that, **the Psalter list is repeated
almost word for word:**

| *How Precious* | *Yaqar* |
|---|---|
| "A ransom nobody can pay. A man in his pomp. The splendour of the pastures, which goes up like smoke." | "A ransom for a life, which no one can afford. A man in his pomp, twice in the same psalm, who will not remain. The splendour of the pastures, which goes up like smoke." |

A reader who meets the second piece will hear the first. Varying the phrasing in one of them
(at least "which goes up like smoke") would remove the echo. Nothing else duplicates.
