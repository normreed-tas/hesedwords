## The exegesis — holds for anything further on Psalm 74

- **v22 `rîbâ rîbekā`** — "contend your contention", cognate accusative. `rîb` is the
  covenant-lawsuit term: the case one party brings against another. God is plaintiff.
  *(Established.)*
- **v9 `'ōtōtênû lō' rā'înû`** — "our signs we do not see." Pairs with v4, where the
  invaders "set up their own signs for signs" (`'ōtōtām 'ōtôt`). Same word both times; most
  English Bibles render the v4 instance "emblems"/"standards", so the two lines cannot be
  heard answering each other. This was the reflection's engine.
- **v2 `qānîtā`** — purchased/acquired (cf. Ex 15:16; Gen 14:19). Property language.
  `shēvet naḥălātekā`, tribe of your inheritance. The suit is over title.
- **vv12–17** — not a digression: `lekā yôm 'ap-lekā laylâ`, the deed read aloud. Creation
  and exodus fused; `gebûlôt`, boundaries, fixed by the one being asked to enforce one. Used
  in the reflection's close: with no result to read, the psalmist argues from a long way
  back.
- **v20 `habbēṭ labberît`** — covenant as the ground of the suit; `ne'ôt ḥāmās`, pastures of
  violence.
- **v21 `dak`** — the crushed. God's cause has a human face; vindication of the Name and
  restoration of the poor are one verdict. *(Reading within the da'at → hesed → mishpat
  frame.)* **Not yet used in either piece — still available.**
- **`zākar` frame** — remember (v2), remember (v18), remember (v22). `miqqedem` in v12
  answers `qedem` in v2: the lament pivots on its own opening word. **Also unused.**

## The cross-text find — the article's spine

There is no word for "cause" in either famous verse.

- **1 Sam 17:29 `halô' dāvār hû'`** — "is it not a dāvār?", a word, a thing, a matter. David
  is answering his brother's accusation that he sneaked down to watch the fighting — *I only
  asked a question.* ESV: "Was it not but a word?" *(Established — standard lexical point on
  `dāvār`; from memory of the lexicon, text not opened.)*
- **Ps 74:22 `rîb`** — a lawsuit, muted into an abstraction.
- **The 1611 defence:** "cause" then could mean a matter, a case, the thing at issue between
  parties — close to `dāvār`, very close to `rîb`. The word was doing honest work. English
  drifted; the translators did not.

## The four registers — the article's method

Four places to stand, not four steps. Deliberately given no headings on the page.

- **English** — accurate, checkable, transmissible. Costs force. The only one doctrine
  builds on.
- **Hebrew/Greek** — the density. Costs access, and makes you feel clever.
- **Twelve-year-old** — the comprehension test. Norm: *"helps me understand."*
- **Eight-year-old** — the transmission test. Costs accuracy, and must. Norm: *"helps me
  explain."* Precedent: `/reflections/telling-davids-census.html`, where he drops the device
  entirely for the Adelaide bedroom.

## Voice lessons from that session

Norm cut five drafted lines, all the same fault — the writer's image rather than his own
speech: *"reading the result backwards"*, *"arrived carrying less than they left with"*,
*"it flatters whoever is holding it"*, *"and I cannot make them into one thing"*, *"I have
been telling the story ever since"*. The last broke his own rule: a sentence that explains
the two before it should replace them, not follow them.

The test that worked: **does the sentence land on an object or on a concept?** *"A room, a
wall, a man standing in the middle of it"* survived where *"you cannot stand outside it"*
did not. *"Makes you feel clever"* survived where *"it flatters whoever is holding it"* did
not.

He also improves lines when handed a plain version — *"came a long way, in time and
language, to reach you"* is his edit and is better than the draft. **When a line is
contested, offer the plain option and let him finish it.** And when the register is wrong he
says so directly (*"too smart and not really mine"*); **the fix is to strip, not to
re-draft**.

He enjoys wordplay in titles (*The Cause Clause*, *Be-cause!*) and accepted the argument
against both — worth holding the line on titles for reflections, which end quietly.

## What was settled in the final pass

- **The `kryptō` link was checked and holds**, and is stronger than the original claim. The
  same root appears three times in Matthew 13: κεκρυμμένα at 13:35 (why Jesus teaches in
  parables at all), κεκρυμμένῳ at 13:44 (the treasure — identical perfect passive
  participle), and ἔκρυψεν at 13:44 (what the finder then does). Three paragraphs in
  Movement Two carry this. Checked against the Greek text, not memory. The article
  deliberately avoids κόσμου at 13:35 ("since the foundation of the world"): that word is a
  textual variant, absent from Sinaiticus and Vaticanus. The field-is-the-world link runs
  through 13:38 instead, which is secure.
- **Ten cuts** removed narrating-the-narrating sentences — lines that told the reader how to
  take the line before them. The pattern to watch: a sentence that promises significance
  instead of showing it.
- **Coda** now opens on *"Glory is never hidden by distance."* The old three-fragment
  opening (*"A field. A clay pot. The person sitting next to you."*) was cut. If it ever
  reads thin, the fix is to reverse the two sentences so the objects arrive first — not to
  restore the fragments.
- **Edwards** named, in Movement Four between the catechism and Liddell. Citation pinned to
  *Miscellanies* no. 448, WJE vol. 13 (Yale, ed. Schafer), 495. **Caveat:** that citation
  came from a source quoting it with the full Yale reference, not from the volume itself.
  The commonly circulated version carries an editorial gloss — *by His glory's being seen
  [known, reflected upon]* — which is not used here.
- **Lewis's closing line:** *vere latitat* confirmed, not "no ordinary people".
- **Pull-quotes, three**, all Norm's own sentences, each set away from its own occurrence:
  *What he did not have was glory with us in it.* (Movement Three) · *You become the weight
  of what you value.* (Movement Four, foreshadowing Jeremiah in Five) · *We ache for a
  country we have never visited.* (Movement Five, before the mud pies).

Length: roughly 2,890 words including scripture quotations and movement headings.

Movement divisions are set as small mono uppercase rules rather than `<h2>`, so the five
movements read as parts of one continuous piece. Reversible if Norm changes his mind.

## Positions settled earlier — do not reopen without new evidence

Glory is the treasure and the pearl, **not people** (but *Christ purchased glory* will not
stand as a flat doctrinal claim — parable image and doctrinal statement are different
registers). *"Multiplied glory"* only in the safe form: nothing is added to God; the one
glory now has a shared form it did not have before. The christological reading is
second-order and the article says so early. Register stays agricultural and domestic, never
financial. Lewis is corrected against the source transcript: he denies the longing is a
memory of Eden, and his definition of glory is being known by God.
