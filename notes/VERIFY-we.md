## 1. The Greek of 3:20

- **"we ask"**: αἰτούμεθα, first person plural, present middle indicative of αἰτέω.
- **"we think"**: νοοῦμεν, first person plural, present active indicative of νοέω.
- **"at work in us"**: κατὰ τὴν δύναμιν τὴν ἐνεργουμένην **ἐν ἡμῖν**. The participle is
  ἐνεργουμένην (present middle/passive of ἐνεργέω, agreeing with "power"). The pronoun is
  ἡμῖν, first person plural dative: "in us" / "among us".
- **3:21**: αὐτῷ ἡ δόξα **ἐν τῇ ἐκκλησίᾳ** καὶ ἐν Χριστῷ Ἰησοῦ, "in the church and in Christ
  Jesus".

Every first-person form in the two verses is plural. There is no singular "I" or "me".

## 2. "Exceeding abundantly"

The lemma is **ὑπερεκπερισσοῦ** (*hyperekperissou*), an adverb. It occurs **three** times in
the NT, all in Paul:

| Reference | Sense |
|---|---|
| Eph 3:20 | able to do far more than we ask |
| 1 Thess 3:10 | praying "most earnestly" night and day |
| 1 Thess 5:13 | esteem them "very highly" (some manuscripts read ὑπερεκπερισσῶς here) |

So it is Pauline-only.

## 3. Chrysostom

It is **Homily 7** on Ephesians. The wording, as on New Advent:

> "For I indeed, says he, pray, but He of Himself, even without any prayer of mine, will do
> works greater than all we ask, not simply greater, nor abundantly greater, but exceeding
> abundantly. And this is evident from the power, that works in us: for neither did we ever
> ask these things, nor did we expect them."

Both lines are **confirmed verbatim**.

**The Gentile claim is not confirmed as stated.** In this passage "these things" has no named
referent. The sentence before it says the proof is "from what the Apostle himself has
written", which means the gifts described in the letter. The same homily does treat the
mystery of the Gentiles earlier (it covers 3:8–21), so the inclusion of the Gentiles is a
fair inference about what Chrysostom has in mind. But he does not say it at 3:20.

**Safe wording:** Chrysostom says God has already done what "we" never asked or expected. If
the piece wants "namely, the inclusion of the Gentiles", it must present that as its own
reading of the homily's context.

## 4. Calvin

Confirmed verbatim (Pringle):

> "Exceeding abundantly above all that we ask or think, is a remarkable expression, and bids
> us entertain no fear lest faith of a proper kind should go to excess."

The next sentence reads: "Whatever expectations we form of Divine blessings, the infinite
goodness of God will exceed all our wishes and all our thoughts."

I read it on Bible Hub's reproduction. The CCEL page address I tried was the wrong section,
so CCEL itself was not opened.

## 5. The Bible Hub "Expository (English Bible)" entry

**Confirmed: Alexander Maclaren**, *Expositions of Holy Scripture*, in the sermon
"Measureless Power and Endless Glory" (Ephesians 3:20). Bible Hub heads it "MacLaren's
Expositions". Both lines are there:

- "One purpose and blessing of faithful prayer is to enlarge the desires which it expresses,
  and to make us think more loftily of the grace to which we appeal."
- "…He can give us more than we think it to be, and more than we ask, when we ask this."

## 6. No other doxology names the church

**The quotation is confirmed.** The NIV Application Commentary sample on Bible Gateway reads:
"No other passage mentions the church explicitly in a doxology, although other doxologies
imply it because Christians are the ones giving the praise."

**The author:** the Bible Gateway page does not name him. The NIVAC Ephesians volume is by
Klyne Snodgrass (1996), from general knowledge. **Confirm the author and page from the
printed book before citing.**

**The independent check confirms it.** I read each doxology in the ESV and searched each
verse for ἐκκλησία in the SBLGNT:

- Rom 11:36; 16:25–27;
- Gal 1:5; Phil 4:20;
- 1 Tim 1:17; 2 Tim 4:18;
- Heb 13:21;
- 1 Pet 4:11; 5:11; 2 Pet 3:18;
- Jude 24–25; Rev 1:6.

None names the church. The word occurs in **Eph 3:21 only**.

Rev 1:6 comes nearest in idea: "made us a kingdom, priests to his God". It uses "us", not
"church".

## 7. Matthew 18:19

- **The verb:** "agree" is συμφωνήσωσιν, from **συμφωνέω** (*symphōneō*).
- **Elsewhere in the NT:** it occurs six times: Matt 18:19; 20:2, 13; Luke 5:36; Acts 5:9;
  15:15.
- **The root:** it is formed from σύμφωνος, "sounding together" (σύν + φωνή), the same root as
  συμφωνία, from which English gets "symphony". Strong's gloss on Bible Hub: "to be
  harmonious".
- **The context is reconciliation.** 18:15 begins "If your brother sins against you", and
  18:20 ends "where two or three are gathered in my name".

## 8. Acts 4

- **4:24:** ὁμοθυμαδόν is there. ESV: "they lifted their voices **together** to God"; KJV:
  "with one accord". It occurs ten times in Acts and once in Paul (Rom 15:6).
- **4:29:** they ask "to continue to speak your word with all **boldness**".
- **4:31:** "the place in which they were gathered together was **shaken**, and they were all
  **filled** with the Holy Spirit and continued to speak the word of God with **boldness**."

They ask for boldness, and they receive a shaking, a filling and boldness.

# PART B — USAGE SURVEY

## Limits (read first)

- **This is an indicative snapshot, not a census.** The sample is 76 items.
- **Most items were classified from a title and a one- or two-line summary** in search
  results, not from the full page. Only the SermonCentral list was opened directly. A sermon
  with a neutral title may be thoroughly individual or thoroughly corporate inside.
- **The "unclear" category is large** (37 of 76) for that reason. Titles such as "He Is
  Able" do not show the application.
- **Source-type bias.** Search engines favour large devotional sites and sermon archives.
- **Social media is not represented.** I could not sample Instagram, Facebook or TikTok.
- **Merchandise is thin.** Etsy and Amazon block automated browsing, so I have 10 products,
  taken from search results.
- **Prosperity and Word of Faith teaching is thin.** A search naming four well-known teachers
  returned nothing tying them to the verse. One clearly financial use was found.
- **The search terms shape the sample.** Some searches used leading terms ("your dreams",
  "breakthrough", "misused"), which pulls in both individual uses and critiques.

**What the sample does and does not show:**

- Devotional sites and verse-of-the-day pages lean individual: "your situation", "your

Notes on the table:

- Devotional and blog titles almost all cite "Ephesians 3:20" alone, though several of the

So v. 20 travels alone mainly in merchandise and in devotional headlines. Preachers more
often keep the two verses together.

## Translation wording

Two wordings dominate popular use:
- **"immeasurably more than all we ask or imagine"** (NIV);
- **"exceedingly abundantly above all that we ask or think"** (NKJV; KJV has "exceeding
  abundantly").

The NLT's "infinitely more than we might ask or think" appears on some prints. The ESV's "far
more abundantly" appears mainly in sermons from Reformed churches. The NIV's "imagine" is the
wording behind "dream" language.

## Published critiques of the "claim it" reading

I found no scholarly article devoted to the point. These are pastoral and popular. I saw them
only as search results unless marked.

| Author / site | Title | Date | Its point |
|---|---|---|---|
| Veritas Domain (blog) | "Not Misapplying Ephesians 3:20" | 17 Mar 2023 | Using it for a prosperity gospel "would be ironic" given the context |
| The Bible Way (church blog) | "Rethinking the Abundance of Ephesians 3:20–21" | 17 Apr 2025 | The context points to "a deeper, more significant abundance" |
| Sermon Academy | "Ephesians 3:20 Meaning: God's Power at Work" | 2026 | Not a promise of "unlimited personal achievement, wealth" |
| Brian Peterson, Working Preacher | Commentary on Ephesians 3:14–21 | c. 2018–19 | "not the fullness promised by a 'prosperity gospel'" |
| Precept Austin | Ephesians 3:20–21 commentary | undated | The benediction is "to him", not "to me" |
| Klyne Snodgrass, NIVAC (**opened**, Bible Gateway sample) | *Ephesians* | 1996 (from general knowledge) | The church is named in the doxology; see A6 |

**One caution from the search itself.** Mainstream commentary also reads "within us" as God's
power in believers, individuals included (so Calvin: "what we feel within ourselves"). The
plural is certain (A1). "Corporate, **not** personal" goes further than the commentators I
saw. "Plural, and for the church's glory" is what the text supports.
