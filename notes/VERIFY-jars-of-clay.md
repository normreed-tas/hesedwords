## 1.3 4:2 and 4:5

**ESV:**
- 4:2: "But we have renounced disgraceful, underhanded ways. We refuse to practice cunning or to
  tamper with God's word…"
- 4:5: "For what we proclaim is not ourselves, but Jesus Christ as Lord…"

**The article:** "We have renounced underhanded ways. We refuse to tamper with God's word. We
do not proclaim ourselves." It drops "disgraceful" and "practice cunning", and turns 4:5
into a negative statement. That is fair compression, and it is not set as a quotation.

## 1.4 The veil

- **Exod 34:29–30, 33.** Moses' face shone; "they were afraid to come near him"; "he put a
  veil over his face".
- **2 Cor 3:7.** "the Israelites could not gaze at Moses' face because of its glory".
- **3:14.** "only through Christ is it taken away".
- **3:16.** "when one turns to the Lord, the veil is removed".

So the article's "Israel could not look at the glory, so the glory was covered up" fits 3:7,
and "in Christ the veil is gone" fits 3:14 and 3:16. One nuance the article does not need:
Paul's own reason for the veil at 3:13 is that they "might not gaze at the outcome of what
was being brought to an end", the fading. The article doesn't contradict it.

## 2.3 *ostrakinos*, "throwaway… the kind you did not mend" — HOLDS

- **In the NT**, the word occurs only twice: 2 Cor 4:7, and 2 Tim 2:20. There, clay vessels
  stand against "vessels of gold and silver… some for honorable use, some for dishonorable".
- **The decisive evidence is in the Torah's purity laws.** The earthen vessel is **broken**,
  where metal or wood is cleaned:

| Verse | Clay | Other material |
|---|---|---|
| **Lev 6:28** | "the earthenware vessel in which it is boiled shall be broken" | a bronze vessel "shall be scoured and rinsed in water" |
| **Lev 11:33** | "you shall break it" | — |
| **Lev 15:12** | "an earthenware vessel that the one with the discharge touches shall be broken" | "every vessel of wood shall be rinsed in water" |

- **The Greek of Lev 15:12 (LXX)** confirms the same word: σκεῦος ὀστράκινον… συντριβήσεται,
  "an earthen vessel… shall be broken".
- **"Did not mend."** Jer 19:11: "as one breaks a potter's vessel, so that it can never be
  mended".

So "throwaway, the kind you did not mend" stands. The true thing underneath is sharper than
the brief feared: **the Law itself treated the clay pot as the one you smash rather than
clean.** (The usual explanation, that porous clay cannot be washed out, is Ellicott's, as
the fetch reported. Not checked further.)

Norm might want that Leviticus line one day. It is not needed for the sentence to stand.
