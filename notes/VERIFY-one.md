**"In us all" (4:6).** The SBLGNT reads ἐν πᾶσιν, "in all". "Us" (ἡμῖν) is a later reading, which the WEB follows; the Expositor's Greek Testament says it "must be omitted". The page leans on "us" once. Norm chose to add a footnote saying so (footnote 3).

## Claims and attributions

**19. The Shema twice a day.** **Supported** for the practice. Josephus, *Antiquities* 4.8.13 (about AD 93): "twice every day; both when the day begins, and when the hour of sleep comes on". Mishnah Berakhot 1:1–2 sets the times for the evening and the morning Shema and names first-century teachers (Rabbi Eliezer, Rabban Gamliel). But the Mishnah (Berakhot 3:3, not opened here) exempts children from the duty, so "a Jewish child… grew up saying" overstated it. Norm chose "grew up hearing".

**20. "Some commentators think Paul may be using words the churches already knew."** Only one commentator could be opened: Ellicott's *Commentary for English Readers*, on 4:4, which says the passage "marks out an embryo creed". That supports creed-like, not already known. Norm chose to soften the sentence to Ellicott, with a footnote (footnote 2).

**21. "Readers have taken it both ways."** **Supported on both sides.** Meyer takes all the forms as masculine (people) and names those who took them as neuter (Erasmus, Rückert and others; Matthies of "God to the world and mankind"). Bengel and the Expositor's Greek Testament also read masculine, and the latter argues for "the special reference to the Church". The paragraph stands.
