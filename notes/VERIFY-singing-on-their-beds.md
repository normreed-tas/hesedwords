## 2.1 *pipiyyot*

- **The form.** פִּיפִיּוֹת is the plural of *pipiyyah*, which is *peh* ("mouth") doubled.
  Strong's gives "edges, borders, mouths", from 6366, "mouth". It occurs twice, in Ps 149:6
  and Isa 41:15.
- **"Two" is not in the word.** The literal sense is "a sword of mouths", meaning edges.
  KJV "twoedged" and ESV "two-edged" are the conventional renderings.
- **I did not open BDB for this word**; Bible Hub has no BDB entry for it.

**Suggested wording:** "The Hebrew behind 'two-edged' is *pipiyyot* — a sword of mouths."
The next sentence, "The sword has mouths of its own", already says it that way, so the fix
tightens rather than changes the beat. It is one word in Norm's locked prose, so it waits
for him.

## 3.3b "Six verses of praise"

Vv. 1–5 are praise. V. 6 begins with praise ("the high praises of God be in their throats")
and turns to the sword in its second line.

So "six verses of praise, and then iron" is only true if v. 6 is counted as praise.
Suggested wording: "**Five verses** of praise, and then iron". Or keep "six" and make the
turn the second half of v. 6. Norm's call.

## 4.1 Said every morning — CONFIRMED

This is from the siddur, not a summary. Sefaria's *Siddur Ashkenaz* has **Ashrei
(Ps 145), Psalms 146, 147, 148, 149 and 150** as consecutive sections of *Pesukei
Dezimra*.

That sequence appears under both:
- **Weekday › Shacharit › Pesukei Dezimra**; and
- **Shabbat › Shacharit › Pesukei Dezimra**.

I opened the text of the weekday Psalm 149 section, "Siddur Ashkenaz, Weekday, Shacharit,
Pesukei Dezimra, Psalm 149". It begins הַלְלוּיָהּ שִֽׁירוּ לַיהוָֹה שִׁיר חָדָשׁ, translated
"Praise God. Sing a new song to Adonoy".

So "those closing psalms are said every morning" holds, weekdays and Shabbat alike. That
is the Ashkenazi rite. I did not open the Sephardi order, but the sentence says "Jewish
practice", not a particular rite, and this is the common core.
