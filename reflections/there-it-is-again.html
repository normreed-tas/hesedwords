Psalm 21:7
25 Nov 2026
Norm Reed

There It Is Again

See also Gates, on Psalm 9:13–14.

Psalm 21 was my reading this morning. It is a king’s psalm. For six verses David tells God what he asked for and what he was given.

You have given him his heart’s desire.

You set a crown of fine gold upon his head.

He asked life of you; you gave it to him.

It is a long list, and all of it is good.

Then verse 7 stopped me.

For the king trusts in the LORD,
and through the steadfast love of the Most High he shall not be moved.

The crown is not what holds him steady. Nor is the long life, or anything else on the list. He is not moved because of God’s steadfast love.

After this verse the psalm turns to the king’s enemies. The trouble is not over, and the steadfast love is what he takes into it.

I do not know Hebrew. But this year I have been learning one Hebrew word, hesed. It is the word underneath “steadfast love” in this verse. Other Bibles call it “lovingkindness” or “mercy”.

It is like a bird you have heard outside your window all your life. One day someone tells you its name. After that you hear it everywhere. The bird was always singing. Now you know what to call it.

That is how it has been for me with this word. I knew the thing before I knew its name. Now when I meet it in my reading I say, there it is again.

On the thirteenth of February my heart stopped. I am here because there were people with me who did CPR. None of it was my doing.

When I ask why I am still alive, I have one answer. It is God’s steadfast love. Hesed is the word for it.

There is a man I visit from time to time. He has not been able to get to church for many years, so I take communion to him. He loves Jesus. He has been through things I could not begin to imagine, and he is thankful to God for what he has.

When people tell me how hard their lives are, I am tempted to tell them about him. I don’t.

Sometimes our talk drifts to money. He wants me to take what I have out of the bank and put it into gold or silver. He is like a dog with a bone about it.

I listen, and yesterday I gently pushed back. I told him my security did not rest in what happened to my money. I said that after the thirteenth of February even the most secure investment would not matter in the slightest. Since that date I have come to know that God’s steadfast love, his hesed, is the only thing of value.

I know what David means.
