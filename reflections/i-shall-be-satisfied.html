A Hesedwords Reflection

I Shall Be Satisfied

A reflection on Psalm 17:15

✦   ✦   ✦

Norm Reed · June 2026

There is a verb in this psalm I keep turning over, the way you turn a stone in your pocket without quite deciding to put it down.

David is being hunted. Men who wish him dead are circling, and the maddening detail is that they are thriving while they do it — full houses, full quivers, nothing in their circumstances suggesting they have anything to answer for. He could ask God to even the score. He asks for something else.

You fill their womb with treasure; they are satisfied with children, and they leave their abundance to their infants. As for me, I shall behold your face in righteousness; when I awake, I shall be satisfied with your likeness.

Psalm 17:14–15 · ESV

Satisfied. The same word, twice, in two verses. Their bellies are full — sava. He will be full — sava — of something else entirely. I had always read that as a tidy contrast and moved on. It is only lately that I have stopped to ask what kind of statement “I shall be satisfied” actually is.

Because it is not quite the plain future it looks like in English. The verb carries an extra letter in the Hebrew — a small grammatical flourish that turns it from prediction into resolve. Not this will happen to me so much as I have decided this is what will fill me. David is not forecasting his own contentment. He is choosing, in the middle of a circumstance that gives him every reason to choose otherwise, what he will let satisfy him.

I notice how much of my own praying has been the first kind and not the second. Reporting a hoped-for outcome rather than declaring an aimed-at one. There is a difference between asking God to make me content and telling him what I have decided will content me — and the second is harder, because it commits you before the circumstances have cooperated.

The other word worth sitting with is likeness — temunah. It is not a common word. It is the word used of what Moses alone was shown of God, and the word used to explain why Israel saw none of it at Horeb, only heard a voice. David is reaching, quietly, for something that was supposed to be exceptional. He does not ask to be told about God. He asks to see what Moses saw.

I find I want to make David’s choice and not just admire it. To decide, ahead of whatever circumstance is doing the hunting at the time, what I will let fill me. Not the full house. The face.

There is one more word in the verse I have not let go of — when I awake. It sent me further than I expected.
