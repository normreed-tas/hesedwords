Norm Reed

Asking for Bread

Companion piece: Seeking.

They got into the boats and went to Capernaum, seeking him.

The boats had come over from Tiberias. The crowd had watched him feed them the day before, on the far side, sitting on the grass. Then he was gone, and the disciples were gone, and the boats were there, so they crossed the water.

When they found him they asked, Rabbi, when did you come here?

He does not answer. There is nothing about the crossing or the timing. He answers something they had not asked.

Truly, truly, I say to you, you are seeking me — not because you saw signs, but because you ate your fill of the loaves.

He does not tell them to stop.

He does not say they were wrong to cross the water, or that they should have stayed home. The seeking stands. He lets it stand, and then he tells them what is underneath it.

Earlier John says there was plenty of grass in that place, and they sat down on it. The word Jesus uses for ate your fill comes from that same word for grass. John does not use either of them anywhere else in the book.

He had said something like this before, to the teachers of the law. You search the Scriptures, he told them, because you think that in them you have eternal life. And it is these that bear witness about me, and you are not willing to come to me that you may have life. Those men worked hard at it. They read and they searched, and he told them what they were searching for.

Then they asked him the question people ask.

What must we do, to be doing the works of God?

Works. More than one, and something to get on with. Tell us what they are and we will do them.

This is the work of God, that you believe in him whom he has sent.

One thing, not a list. And not work done for God — the work of God. The believing he asks for is not agreeing that something about him is true. It is trusting a person, the way you trust someone you know: that this one was sent, and that the Father is the one who sent him.

He keeps going, and it gets harder.

He tells them he is the bread that came down from heaven. He tells them they must eat his flesh and drink his blood, and that whoever does will live.

They wanted something from him, and he kept making the answer be himself.

The people who had crossed a lake to find him heard enough.

This is a hard saying; who can listen to it?

And they went back, and walked with him no more.

He turns to the twelve and asks whether they want to go as well.

Lord, to whom shall we go? You have the words of eternal life.

Peter does not say he understands it. He says there is nowhere else to go.

A couple of days after I decided to follow Jesus, I was lying in bed and I said my prayers before I went to sleep. It was something I did from time to time. There were words I had heard over the years and I mumbled them quietly and closed my eyes. It was the right thing to do, and it seemed more important now.

I had barely finished when God spoke to me. Not out loud. It was the sort of inward prodding I have since learned to recognise.

What did you just say?

I said my prayers.

Yes. But what did you say?

The conversation went on for a few minutes, and by the end of it I was praying something I had not planned to pray. Lord, teach me to pray.

I knew those words were in the Bible somewhere. At the time I was working in the Department of Defence, and there was a Bible study running there. The subject that term was prayer. A couple of weekends later a seminar came to town called the Change the World School of Prayer, and it turned my life over.

I learned how to pray, and when, and what prayer was. That was fundamental to everything after it. This morning I am not so sure I understood why.

Selah

I find myself asking what I am seeking him for. I sit in prayer meetings. What I hear, most of the time, is people asking for bread. So do I.
