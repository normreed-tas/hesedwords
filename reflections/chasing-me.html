Psalm 23:6
28 Nov 2026
Norm Reed

Chasing Me

The morning itself is told in Coincidence. The same Hebrew verb, in Ecclesiastes 3:15: God Seeks What Has Been Driven Away.

Surely goodness and mercy shall follow me all the days of my life, and I shall dwell in the house of the LORD forever. (Psalm 23:6)

I have always pictured this psalm being written on a hillside: a boy out with his father’s sheep, with grass and still water and a staff in his hand, putting into words what he could see around him.

I heard the last verse the same way. Goodness and mercy came along behind him the way the sheep came along behind a shepherd, keeping pace, never far off and never in a hurry.

It must be one of the most preached verses in the Bible. That is how I heard it every time.

· · ·

The Hebrew word behind follow is radaph. It does not mean walking along behind someone. It means to pursue, to chase, to run someone down. It is the word for an army going after a fleeing enemy. Pharaoh’s chariots did it to Israel, all the way to the edge of the sea.

David knew that word from the inside. For years Saul hunted him through the wilderness. When David finally called across to him from the top of a hill, with a wide space between them, that was the word he used: Why does my lord pursue after his servant?

David knew what it was to hear someone coming after him. At the end of his psalm, he chose that word for goodness and mercy.

· · ·

On the thirteenth of February my heart stopped. It was a little after seven in the morning, in the church café. I was early for a meeting, and I had taken a few steps to make a coffee when I went down. I have told the rest in Coincidence: who walked in, and when.

What I did not see then was that they had got there first. Ian, Peter and Tom had all come through the door before I fell. By the time I hit the floor, the people who would keep me alive were already standing in the room.

The first word in the verse is goodness. For me, even my death was good. It happened in a place where it could be survived.

· · ·

The second word is mercy, and underneath it is hesed: God’s steadfast, covenant love, the word I have been learning ever since.

I did not choose the time or the place. I did not arrange who would come through the door. It was all of God, and none of it was me.

I fell, and he was right behind me.

The psalm says goodness and mercy will pursue me. It does not say they will catch me. That morning the pursuit closed, and I fell into his arms.

· · ·

Two verses earlier, David is in the valley: Even though I walk through the valley of the shadow of death, I will fear no evil, for you are with me.

Up to that line he has been talking about God: he makes me lie down, he leads me, he restores my soul. In the valley he starts talking to him: you are with me.

In Hebrew there is no verb in that phrase, only attah immadi: you, with me. It does not say God came, or arrived, or did anything. It says where he is.

He was there before he caught me. He had always been there.

· · ·

All the days of my life. That takes in the days before the thirteenth of February, when I did not know anyone was behind me, and every day since.

I do not know how many more there will be. I know who is behind me.
