How Precious

Companion: Yaqar, a word study on what the Bible calls precious, and who is doing the valuing.

In church yesterday, when time was given for people to pray, one of the congregation read from Psalm 36. Not as a lesson. It was prayer, and the psalm was what he had to say.

What stood out was hesed.

His name is Ross. After the service I went and asked him where the verses were.

Up to that point the psalm is all big things. Heavens. Clouds. Mountains. The great deep. Then verse 7 comes down to a person under a wing.

How precious is your steadfast love, O God! The children of mankind take refuge in the shadow of your wings.

Precious is a strange word to find there. It is not a word about how something feels. It is a word about what something is worth.

The Hebrew is yaqar. It is the word for the precious stones the Queen of Sheba brought Solomon. It is what Proverbs says wisdom is more of than jewels. A valuer’s word. You would use it standing over a tray with a glass in your eye.

In the Psalms it is used of a ransom nobody can pay, of a man in his pomp, of the splendour of the pastures that vanishes.

Then verse 7, and the word is used of God’s hesed.

I stumbled on this word shortly after my cardiac arrest. I have been collecting it ever since.

A man finds a nugget. After that he is not a man who found gold once. He is a man who looks at ground differently — every creek bed, every cut bank, every heap somebody else walked past. The nugget changed what he does with his eyes.

That is what the word has done to my reading.

I woke this morning with it still going. Precious. And what came was not the psalm. It was Gollum.

My precious.1 He is not wrong about the ring. It is worth what he says it is worth. It gave him things. It also took him apart, slowly, over a long time, until there was not much of him left that was not about the ring.

The ring let itself be held. That was the lie in it.

Hesed does not let itself be held, and the psalm never asks you to. The verse says the children of mankind take refuge in the shadow of your wings. Not take hold. Take refuge. You go underneath it.

Both men end up held by the thing they prized. Gollum went under the ring. The psalmist goes under the wings.

Hesed is slowly becoming precious to me.

1 J. R. R. Tolkien, The Hobbit (1937), ch. 5, “Riddles in the Dark”; The Lord of the Rings, The Fellowship of the Ring, book I, ch. 2, “The Shadow of the Past”. ↩
