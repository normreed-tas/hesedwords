1 Corinthians 1–2
28 Oct 2026
Norm Reed

Maturity?

Corinth was a new city. Rome had rebuilt it about a hundred years before Paul wrote, and it filled up with traders and freed slaves. Hardly anyone had an old family name to stand on, so you got your standing from who you were attached to. That is what I follow Paul and I follow Apollos are about. Not teaching. Position.

The opening of the letter is clever. Paul thanks God for four things the Corinthians are good at: talking, knowing things, spiritual gifts, and waiting for Jesus to come back. Then the rest of the letter is about how they’ve got all four wrong. He isn’t being sarcastic. He means it. He just lists their best things first, and spends the rest of the book sorting them out.

Paul is writing to a church that has got full of itself. They think some Christians have graduated to a deeper, secret level — the insiders, the ones who really get it.

So Paul says: all right. There is a secret wisdom, and we do tell it to the grown-up ones.

And then the secret turns out to be Jesus on a cross.

That’s it. That’s the whole secret. Not a hidden teaching you unlock after years. The most public execution in the city, in broad daylight, where anyone could watch. The men who ran the world looked straight at it and saw a failed troublemaker. They had no idea who they were killing. That’s why Paul calls it hidden. It was in plain sight, and nobody with power could read it.

Then Paul lets them lean in for the secret, and the secret is the thing they were already trying to climb past. He says this wisdom is for the mature ones — and a few lines later tells the Corinthians they are still babies.

On glory: they wanted to look impressive. Paul says God planned glory for them before the world started — and then calls Jesus “the Lord of glory” while describing him being killed. Same word. So the glory they’re chasing is real, but it comes by the road Jesus took, not around it.

The word mature invites you in. That is how the sentence is built. And leaning in is not the mistake — the Corinthians leaned in too. They thought they had already arrived, already rich, already reigning like kings. Nobody could tell them they were babies.

Selah

I read these two chapters this morning. The word that kept coming up was wisdom, and I stopped at the one Paul says he tells to the mature. I put myself in. I did not expect what came next.
