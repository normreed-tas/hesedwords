Psalm 119
4 Oct 2026
Norm Reed

According to Your Steadfast Love

Psalm 119 is the longest psalm in the Bible. It has 176 verses, set out in twenty-two groups of eight. Each group belongs to one letter of the Hebrew alphabet, and every verse in the group begins with that letter. It works through the alphabet from start to finish.

Nearly every verse names God’s law in some way. The psalmist uses eight or so words for it: law, testimonies, precepts, statutes, commandments, rules, word and promise. He moves from one to the next and back again, all the way through.

So most of us think of Psalm 119 as the law psalm. That’s fair. It is a long song of love for what God has said.

But another word runs through it as well. It comes up only seven times, and you could read the whole psalm without noticing it.

The word is hesed. Our English Bibles translate it in different ways: steadfast love, lovingkindness, mercy. It is the love that God binds himself to and does not let go of. It is the love that keeps faith.

Hesed comes up seven times in Psalm 119. Each time it is “your hesed,” God’s and no one else’s. Each time the psalmist is speaking to God, not about him.

He never stops to explain hesed or praise it at length. He doesn’t build a stanza around it. It turns up when he asks for something.

“Let your steadfast love come to me, O LORD” (v. 41).

“Let your steadfast love comfort me” (v. 76).

“Deal with your servant according to your steadfast love” (v. 124).

The law runs all through the psalm. Hesed comes in when he prays.

Psalm 119 keeps using one small phrase: “according to your …”

“Give me life, O LORD, according to your word” (v. 107).

“Be gracious to me according to your promise” (v. 58).

He asks God to act, and then he names what God should act by. Mostly it is God’s word or God’s promise. He is holding God to what God has said.

Four times the thing he names is God’s hesed: “according to your steadfast love.” Hesed takes its place alongside God’s word and God’s promise, as something God can be held to.

In one verse he puts hesed and God’s justice side by side:

“Hear my voice according to your steadfast love; O LORD, according to your justice give me life” (v. 149).

He doesn’t set mercy against justice, or choose one over the other. He asks by both.

When the psalmist asks for hesed, he asks for three things.

He asks for life. “Give me life, O LORD, according to your steadfast love” (v. 159). He prays this twice by God’s hesed, and once by his hesed and his justice together (v. 149).

He asks to be taught. “Deal with your servant according to your steadfast love, and teach me your statutes” (v. 124). He asks the same in v. 64.

He asks for comfort. “Let your steadfast love comfort me according to your promise to your servant” (v. 76).

One verse puts it all in order:

“In your steadfast love give me life, that I may keep the testimonies of your mouth” (v. 88).

He doesn’t keep the law so that God will love him. He asks God’s love to give him life so that he can keep the law. The love comes first. The keeping follows.

Near the end of the psalm he says, “Consider how I love your precepts!” (v. 159). He means it. He has spent most of the psalm showing it.

But he doesn’t ask God to reward that love. He asks for hesed: “Give me life, O LORD, according to your steadfast love.”

Then the psalm ends like this:

“I have gone astray like a lost sheep; seek your servant, for I do not forget your commandments” (v. 176).

He still loves God’s law. He has not forgotten it. And he is still lost. The longest psalm about God’s word doesn’t end with a man standing firm. It ends with a sheep that has wandered off, asking the shepherd to come and find him.

That is where hesed has been all along. It goes looking for the one who wandered.

A prayer from Psalm 119

Let your steadfast love come to me, O LORD,
your salvation according to your promise.
The earth, O LORD, is full of your steadfast love;
teach me your statutes!
Let your steadfast love comfort me
according to your promise to your servant.
In your steadfast love give me life,1
that I may keep the testimonies of your mouth.
Deal with your servant according to your steadfast love,
and teach me your statutes.
Hear my voice according to your steadfast love;
O LORD, according to your justice give me life.
Consider how I love your precepts!
Give me life, O LORD, according to your steadfast love.
I have gone astray like a lost sheep;
seek your servant,
for I do not forget your commandments.

Psalm 119:41, 64, 76, 88, 124, 149, 159, 176 (ESV)

1 In the Hebrew, verse 88 opens with the phrase that verses 124, 149 and 159 also use: “according to your hesed.” The ESV translates it “in your steadfast love” here. ↩
