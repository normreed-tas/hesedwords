Pastoral Writing

God Is Searching

Lessons from the life of King Asa

A good king meets two threats. The first, a million men at Zephathah, he hands to God, and is told his work shall be rewarded. The second, a wall at Ramah, he solves himself with silver and gold, cleanly and successfully, and is told he has done foolishly. Written by a man who fixes problems for a living, about being told to wait, and about the eyes of the LORD that run to and fro throughout the whole earth.

For Younger Readers

Therefore

Romans 12 in seven days, for a young teenager

For eleven chapters Paul explains what God has done. Then chapter twelve begins with therefore, and turns to what a person looks like once they have understood it. Sunday is the part God did, and you cannot fail it. The other six days are a mirror, not a list of jobs: being right about yourself, what your gifts are for, love that isn't an act, the people who are hardest to be near, getting even, and not letting cruelty make you like it. Every day has a page to write or draw on.

For Younger Readers

The Songs for Every Day

Seven psalms, one for each day of the week

A very long time ago there was a temple, and every morning and every evening something happened there — when people were happy and when they were sad, when everything was going well and when everything had gone wrong. They called it the tamid: the one that keeps going. And while it happened, the singers sang. Here are those seven songs, retold for younger readers, each with something to wonder about and a prayer you can say.

For Daily Reading

The Tamid Psalms

The seven psalms the Levites sang beside the continual offering

Twice a day, whether the nation was faithful or faithless, a lamb went up — the tamid, the continual offering. While it burned the Levites sang, and the Mishnah records which psalm belonged to which day. Here are those seven, one for each day of the week, each with a short note on why it sat where it did. Not a study guide. A pattern for reading, until they are under your breath rather than in front of your eyes.

A Man Who Runs

John Mark, Rome, the winter of 66

In a cell under a Roman street, John Mark reads his unfinished Gospel aloud to Paul — the man who once refused to travel with him. Peter is dead. Paul is old and chained. And the chapter Mark has not written is the one about a young man in a garden who left the linen cloth and ran away naked.

Who Would Have Thought

A story from Psalm 19

A girl, a long week at home, and a verse caught by accident. Jess wasn't looking for Psalm 19. But somewhere between the medicine tablets, her gran's folded hands, and a phone bright in the dark, six old lines found her — and turned her around. A story for younger readers, and for anyone who has read past a truth without knowing it was being offered.
